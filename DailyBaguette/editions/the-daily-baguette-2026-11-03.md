**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Tuesday 3 November 2026 — Oil loses 7.6%, Alstom loses 26%, America votes**

Good morning. Yesterday the market got the peace headline it wanted and promptly decided to worry about AI capex instead. Europe, which has the good fortune to own very little AI, had the better day. Today the Americans go to the polls with Treasuries yielding 4.9% and the futures curve pricing a December Fed *hike*. Bonne journée.

**Paris** reclaimed 8,000 as lower oil helped almost everyone except the oil company. **Alstom** (-26.09%) did the rest of the talking: a profit warning cut organic growth guidance to 2-4% from about 5%, adjusted margin to about 1% from 6.5%, and free cash flow to minus €1.5bn. The company blamed industrial difficulties, Coradia Max certification delays and German project execution. **Safran** (+4.53%) topped the CAC after being selected for three inertial navigation programmes for France's nuclear deterrent. **Euronext** (-3.49%) fell on the departure of CEO Stéphane Boujnah, with NN Group's David Knibbe to take over at the May 2027 AGM. **TotalEnergies** lost over 2%. Lower fuel costs took **Ryanair** up 5.24% in Dublin.

**Wall Street** was two markets. Equal-weight S&P rose 0.76% and breadth was firmly positive, yet the headline fell, because Technology (-2.95%) and Energy (-3.96%) dragged while Consumer Discretionary (+2.41%) and Financials (+1.86%) rallied. The S&P had been up as much as 1.1% in the morning before fading.

- **Marvell** (-12.7%) and **Lumentum** (-18.6%): Marvell's all-share deal for Lumentum, at 4.5 MRVL per LITE, was received as an expensive vote of confidence in AI optics.
- **Oracle** (-8.8%): plans to sell $10bn of stock and $10bn of bonds to fund its AI buildout. Its 5-year CDS stands at 292bp.
- **AMD** -7.5%, **Dell** -7.4%, **Seagate** -7.9%: guilt by association.
- **IDEXX** +21.9% on a raised 2026 outlook; **Sherwin-Williams** +5.6% and **Home Depot** +3.3% lifted the Dow; airlines rose around 4-5% on cheaper fuel.

**After the bell:** **Apple** closed down 1.83% at $338.32, then dropped a further c.2.8% in after-hours trade despite $114.5bn of revenue against a $113.4bn estimate, because the December-quarter margin outlook and iPad sales disappointed. **Palantir** grew revenue 96% to $2.31bn, raised its forecast, and fell about 3% after hours. Such is the price of perfection.

### Bonds

- **US Treasuries:** 10-year about 4.89% (-3.5bp), 2-year about 4.58%, 30-year about 5.27% (-4.1bp). Trading Economics shows 4.91% and 4.61% for the 10- and 2-year, so treat the levels as good to a couple of basis points.
- **Europe:** OAT 10-year 4.91% (from 4.93%), Bund 3.31% (from 3.34%), on Banque de France/Bundesbank-based data. Trading Economics has 4.90% and 3.29%.
- **OAT-Bund spread:** 159.9bp, up 0.9bp on the day (about 161bp on the Trading Economics pair). The French 10-year now sits level with the US Treasury, and Trading Economics has Italy at 4.41%, well inside France. Boursorama's wrap also notes Fitch holding France at A with a negative outlook.

### FX

**EUR/USD** about 1.119, **GBP/USD** about 1.341, **USD/JPY** about 157.9, **DXY** about 101.77. Daily moves were small, and our snapshots disagree on their sign, so we won't dress them up. The mover came this morning: **AUD/USD** slipped 0.3% to 69.76 US cents after the RBA hiked 25bp to 4.85%, on a 6-3 vote and with the tightening bias removed from the statement. That was its fifth hike this year, and the market read it as dovish.

### Other

- **Brent** settled -7.63% at $104.77 and **WTI** -7.36% at $96.46, the sharpest fall in about three months. It stands at about $105.50 this morning (+0.7%).
- **Gold** rose about 1.5% to about $3,957 as lower oil trimmed inflation fears, then gave back 0.6% to about $3,922 this morning. It hit a one-year low of $3,870 last Friday.
- **Silver** (-2.8%) and **copper** (-2.4%) were lower in early trade.
- **Bitcoin** about $76,600 this morning, against about $78,500 quoted yesterday.

### The why

Oil did the work. Trump reportedly told Netanyahu he wanted talks with Iran to run their course (Axios, via BFM Bourse), Rubio spoke of a peaceful resolution, Iran's leadership gave its government more room to negotiate, and Qatari-mediated talks produced a draft for reopening Hormuz. A 7.6% drop in crude is a tax cut for Europe and for anyone who flies. Tech, meanwhile, is now being asked who pays for all this: the Oracle and Marvell news kept AI financing, not AI demand, at the centre of the debate. Data was mixed: ISM manufacturing beat at 55.5 (consensus about 54.5, production at its highest since 2021, prices paid easing to 75.2), but construction spending fell 1.9% against an expected +0.4%, its steepest drop since April 2020.

## The fun bit

Election Day was once a market holiday. US exchanges shut on presidential Election Days through 1980, and Nasdaq first traded through one on 6 November 1984, in its 13th year. So today's traders vote before work, or after, or between AMD scenarios.

Meanwhile, in Australia, the RBA took rates to an 18-year high on Melbourne Cup Day, the race known as the one that stops the nation. It did not stop the Board, though three of its nine members would have preferred to hold.
