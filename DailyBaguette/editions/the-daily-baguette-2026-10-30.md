**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Friday 30 October 2026 — Lagarde exits stage left, Alphabet takes a Black Tuesday bow**

Good morning,

Thursday had two halves. Europe took an ECB hold, some solid earnings and a surprise resignation, and closed near seven-week highs. Wall Street then spent the evening repricing what AI capex costs, and Alphabet had the worst day in its history. Friday brings a batch of French data, Big Oil earnings and a mood that will be tested from 9:00.

**Paris led on earnings.** The CAC came within 30 points of 8,000 (intraday high 7,998).
- **Renault** gained about 6.9%, the best performer in the index. Q3 revenue beat expectations and the group confirmed its guidance.
- **Airbus** rose 2.3% and **Safran** 2.6%. Airbus confirmed its 2026 targets even though Q3 results fell and it burned cash.
- **Air Liquide** added 1.8%. Q3 sales were €7.26bn against €7.16bn expected, and it announced a first €1bn buyback tranche.
- **Société Générale** edged up 0.7% on record quarterly net income of €1.89bn (+24%).
- **TotalEnergies** rose 1.2%, helped by its LNG arm.
- **Dassault Systèmes** gained more than 6% as analysts raised their targets.
- Off-index, **Lectra** (−20%) and **Pluxee** (−16%) both disappointed on outlook.

**Wall Street split in two.** Breadth was positive: the equal-weight S&P rose 1.2% and healthcare gained 2.4%. Mega-cap tech dragged the indices down.
- **Alphabet −12.6%**, its worst session on record. Q3 EPS of $2.12 badly missed the $2.94 consensus. Cloud revenue roughly doubled to $30.7bn, but free cash flow was negative (about −$9bn) because of AI capex, and long-term debt rose to $125bn from $22bn a year earlier.
- **Microsoft** ended flat (−0.2%) after an early cash-flow-driven rally faded.
- **Caterpillar −9.3%**, its worst day since March 2020, on weak construction sales.
- **Eli Lilly +6.5%**: revenue up 39% and guidance raised.
- **ServiceNow −27%**, **Fortinet −20%** and **Marvell −13%** (Bloomberg reported Marvell is in advanced talks to buy Lumentum).
- In M&A, **ADT +33%** after agreeing to be bought by Thrive Holdings for $10 a share, and **DoorDash +5.6%** on its agreed purchase of Instacart at $65 a share.

**After hours it got worse.** **Intel** fell roughly 17–23% (sources differ) after guiding Q4 revenue below Q3 and warning that the PC slowdown will last into H1 2027. **Amazon** lost about 6–7%: AWS grew 39%, but trailing free cash flow turned negative. **Roblox, Twilio and Reddit** each dropped more than 15%. At 6pm New York time, Nasdaq-100 futures were down about 1.5%.

### Bonds

- **US Treasuries rallied** on growth worries rather than inflation relief. The 10-year closed around **4.98% (−7bp)**, back under 5%, and the 2-year at **4.61% (−6bp)**, per the Treasury curve via CappNotes. Investopedia had the 10-year nearer 4.95%. In Asia this morning it trades around **4.90%**, a one-month low (Reuters).
- **Bunds:** the 10-year was at 3.355% before the decision (2-year 3.068%) and slipped toward **3.3%** afterwards, its lowest since late August (Investing.com, FX.co). We did not find an exact closing level.
- **OAT:** the French 10-year went **below 4.9%** after the ECB, from 4.95% on Wednesday (Trading Economics). It traded at nearly 5.14% a week ago. Using these intraday levels, the **OAT-Bund spread is about 160bp**. That is our calculation, not a quoted close, and it remains a very wide gap for a core-ish sovereign.

### FX

- **EUR/USD** fell to about **$1.113** after the ECB, its lowest since May 2025 (Reuters). Paris quoted $1.1164 at the close (BFM Bourse). It has recovered to **$1.1197** in Asia this morning, but the pair is still heading for a monthly loss of about 1.2%.
- **DXY** closed −0.3% at 101.87, and trades at 101.77 this morning.
- **USD/JPY** dropped to about **157.1** as the yen firmed after the BoJ (see below).

### Other

- **Brent** settled at **$114.88 (−0.4%)** and **WTI** at $102.00 (−0.8%) on Iran / Strait of Hormuz diplomacy (CappNotes). Paris-afternoon quotes on the December contract were lower ($110.15, −1.3%, BFM), so watch which contract your screen shows.
- **Gold** −0.5% to $4,031.70/oz. **Silver** −2.4%.
- **Bitcoin** −2.9% to about $78,900. **Ether** −4.1% to about $2,260. Crypto traded like high-beta tech, as usual.

### The "why"

1. **ECB on hold, door open for December.** Rates stayed at 2.50% (deposit). The Governing Council said energy-driven inflation risks are rising, and September inflation was 3.8%. Markets price about 23bp of tightening by December (Trading Economics), roughly 80% odds of a hike (Investing.com), and about 70bp in total by end-2027.
2. **Lagarde resigns early.** She will leave on **7 February 2027**, reportedly to chair the World Economic Forum. With half the Executive Board also changing in H1 2027, succession politics now matter for pricing, and France's debt is a sensitive subject in that debate. The euro and short-end yields fell.
3. **The Fed's hold on Wednesday** (3.75–4.00%, vote 10–2) was read as hawkish. CME FedWatch puts a December hike at roughly **78–80%**.
4. **US data was mixed for bonds.** Q3 GDP came in at **+3.4%** annualised (consensus 3.1%) and jobless claims fell to 194k. September headline PCE was +0.5% m/m and +3.6% y/y, and core was +0.3% m/m, +3.1% y/y, in line. The bond rally was driven by AI-capex worries, not this data.
5. **AI capex has a cost.** Alphabet's and Amazon's results put negative free cash flow and rising debt on the front page. The market is asking for returns on the spending, not just bigger spending plans.

## Today — what might happen

- **European tech opens lower: our call ~75%.** Overnight US tech futures and the Intel guidance set the tone. Whether the CAC stays above 7,900 depends on Sanofi and Crédit Agricole more than on chips.
- **French data decides OAT direction.** A firm October CPI print would reinforce the ~80% December ECB hike pricing (Investing.com). A soft GDP number would put fiscal concerns back on the table. *Our call:* 10-year OAT stays in a 4.80–4.95% range today, ~65%.

- **Big Oil beats.** With Brent above $110 all quarter, an EPS beat at Exxon/Chevron is more likely than not (*our call: ~65%*). Buyback commentary will move the shares more than the beat itself.
- **Fed December hike:** CME FedWatch ~78–80%. Chicago PMI won't change that. Next Friday's payrolls might.
- **Month-end rebalancing.** After a month in which bonds rallied and mega-caps fell, expect noisy flows into the close.

## The fun bit

Yesterday was **29 October**, the anniversary of **Black Tuesday (1929)**, when the Dow fell about 12% in one session. Ninety-seven years later, Alphabet lost **12.6%** on the same date, its worst day as a public company, and roughly $500bn in market value. The difference is that in 1929 the ticker tape ran hours behind. On Thursday, the selling started before the CFO had finished the opening remarks on the call.

Have a good weekend, and remember the clocks: Europe changed last Sunday, but the US only switches back this Sunday (1 November). Today is the last day New York opens at 14:30 Paris time; from Monday it is back to 15:30.
