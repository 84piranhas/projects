**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Friday 20 November 2026 — Anthropic pops, everything else drops**

Good morning,

Wall Street finally got its trillion-dollar debutante, and celebrated by selling almost everything else. The S&P 500 logged a sixth straight loss, the Dow lost its 50,000 handle, and Asia has opened Friday in the same mood. Coffee first, then the damage report.

---

**Europe** spent most of the day pretending to be fine, then slid in the last hours as Wall Street turned. Paris stayed near flat for a long time before giving way. The winners had their own stories: **Soitec** +12.0% after strong first-half sales (+37%) and a ~40% annual growth target, **ADP** +3.9% on a supportive regulatory opinion on its 2027-2034 contract, and **STMicro** +1.6%. On the losing side, **Accor** -3.1%, **Capgemini** -2.7% and **Sanofi** -2.9%, the latter on the same day reports said it is looking at buying US biotech Insmed. In Germany, **Evonik** fell about 18% intraday after BASF dropped its takeover approach, wiping out Tuesday's bid premium.

**US**: Anthropic (ANT) priced at $83, opened at $111.50 in a ~$12bn opening cross, and closed at $97.17 (+17.1%). The IPO raised a record $81.3bn, and at the open the market valued the company above $1.5tn. The rest of the AI trade paid for it: retail investors sold AMD and Nvidia to buy ANT (per Vanda Research). **AMD** -7.7% (Hunterbrook disclosed a short position, citing possible chip flows to a blacklisted Chinese server maker), **Nvidia** -3.3% despite Tuesday's strong quarter, **Amazon** about -3.9%, **Meta** about -3.3%. Selling then spread beyond tech, with industrials -2.6%. Only energy and staples ended up. Bright spots: **Insmed** +20.6% on the Sanofi reports, and **Palo Alto Networks** +6.4% on a beat plus a $4.5bn acquisition. ANT slipped to ~$92.60 after hours. The VIX jumped about 16% to 28.4.

**Data**: initial jobless claims came in at 203k (consensus 197k), with continuing claims up sharply. The Philly Fed index dropped to 6.3, a six-month low, against forecasts of roughly 20-23, while its prices-paid component rose. That is not a good mix with a Fed preparing to hike again.

### Bonds

- **US Treasuries**: buyers came in for safety. The 10-year closed around 5.05% (-2bp) and the 2-year around 4.77% (-3bp), according to Investing.com; the official Treasury curve for 19 Nov was not yet published at writing time (the 18 Nov official par yields were 5.07% and 4.80%). That makes four straight declines in the 10-year yield.
- **Europe**: the 10-year OAT at around 4.88% and the 10-year Bund at around 3.38% (indicative market levels, Ideal Investisseur). **OAT-Bund spread: 150 bp** by our calculation, about 3 bp wider on the day. France sold almost €12bn of OATs (2030-2034) at the top of its target range. The Bund curve was not helped by hawkish ECB voices (Nagel, Kocher) suggesting more hikes may be needed.

### FX

- **EUR/USD**: ECB reference rate 1.0992 (vs 1.0968 the day before). The pair briefly went above 1.10 on the ECB comments, then gave it back as risk aversion rose. It ended the session around 1.0967 (Interactive Brokers) and trades near 1.098 this morning.
- **USD/JPY** remains just below 160 (around 159.3 in Asia), close to the level where Tokyo has stepped in before.

### Other

- **Brent** held around $92 on Thursday, supported by IRGC claims that tankers hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, before slipping to about $89.7 in early Asian trading.
- **Gold** fell about 2.1% to ~$3,664 and **silver** fell harder. When everything goes down together, investors sell what they can rather than what they want to.
- **Bitcoin** around $61.5-62k, no help as a hedge either.

### Why it matters

The ANT listing was supposed to restart the AI trade. Instead, it soaked up liquidity: money moved from the old AI winners into the new name, and a market already worried about rates (the October FOMC minutes showed most officials open to more hikes; futures price a December hike as near certain) lost its main support. Add softer US data with rising prices and you get the worst combination for equities: slower growth, but no rate cuts coming.

## Today — what might happen

- **Fed in December**: CME FedWatch (via Investing.com) puts a 25 bp hike at ~94% and a 50 bp hike at ~6%. A weak UMich or high inflation expectations would put the 50 bp case back in discussion.
- **ECB in December**: money markets put ~91% on a 25 bp hike (Trading Economics, as of 18 Nov). Lagarde is unlikely to push back against it this morning. *Our call: ~75% she leaves December pricing unchanged.*
- **European open**: given where US futures are, *our call: ~75% that the CAC 40 opens more than 1% lower*. Options expiry may add volatility around strikes in the afternoon.
- **ANT, day two**: no published probabilities here. After a 17% first-day gain and a weaker after-hours trade, another volatile session looks likely. *Our call: more likely to trade below Thursday's close than above (~60/40).*

## The fun bit

Anthropic's IPO set several records in one session: an $81.3bn raise and an opening cross of about $12bn, and its first 30 minutes of retail net buying reached $18.6m, according to Vanda Research. That was only *second* among 2026 IPOs, behind SpaceX's $25.6m. Meanwhile, the Dow lost 1,101 points the same day. Wall Street at its most consistent: a record raise for one name, a sell-off for the rest.

Have a good weekend, and keep some powder dry — and some butter for the baguette.
