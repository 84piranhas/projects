**Paris.** The CAC recovered from Friday's luxury-led wobble as French long yields fell. Stellantis (+3.8%), Hermès (+3.2%, clawing back part of Friday's post-sales drop), STMicro (+2.6%) and BNP Paribas (+2.4%) led. Banks were the obvious beneficiaries of the Moody's reprieve. At the other end, **GTT plunged 11.7%** to €181.10 after a quarterly update Zonebourse described as unusually disappointing.

**Europe.** The Euro Stoxx 50 bounced off its lowest level since June. Travel was the best sector, with airlines enjoying cheaper jet fuel; media lagged. **Telefónica fell nearly 7%** after a JPMorgan downgrade to Underweight on credit concerns, with its Brazilian exposure not helping after Sunday's election. In Frankfurt, **Bayer rose 3.6%** on Phase III data for prostate-cancer drug Nubeqa and a Chinese approval for blood thinner asundexian; flatexDEGIRO jumped 4.6% on a Barclays thumbs-up. The **Ifo** business climate rose unexpectedly for a sixth straight month to its highest since spring 2023 — not bad for a country paying through the nose for gas.

**Wall Street.** Anthropic's SEC filing to list on the Nasdaq (ticker: ANT) put a bid under AI names: Micron +3%, AMD +2.8%, Nvidia +2.4% (Trading Economics). **Tesla rose 5–6%** after Barclays upgraded it to Overweight ($450 target), citing a possible SpaceX tie-up. **Range Resources soared ~16%** on EQT's all-stock takeover, valued above $50bn including debt. **Meta slipped ~1.9%** on plans to raise up to $95bn in equity to fund AI capex — the market's reminder that someone has to pay for all those GPUs. Neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius, Iren) fell after Elon Musk said many could fail. Brazil's election hammered local fintechs: Nu Holdings −20%, and XP reportedly roughly halved. FICO dropped 8.6% on downgrades.

- **US Treasury 10y: ~4.98–4.99%**, roughly flat to slightly lower (TheStreet / Trading Economics); **2y up to ~4.71–4.75%** — a modest bear flattener ahead of the Fed.

**Why.** On Friday evening Moody's kept France at **Aa3, negative outlook**, when a cut to A1 (in line with Fitch and S&P at A+) had been widely expected. The agency's view rests on the assumption that a deficit-reducing 2027 budget gets passed — the same day, the Assemblée rejected the first part of the social security budget by 115 votes to 1. A reprieve, not a pardon.

## The fun bit

Twenty-nine years ago, on **27 October 1997**, the Dow fell 554.26 points (−7.18%) to 7,161.15 in the Asian-crisis "mini-crash". It was the first time the NYSE's circuit breakers were triggered: a 30-minute halt at 2:36 pm, then trading was stopped altogether at 3:30 pm after the 550-point limit. The next day the Dow rose a record 337 points and recovered 61% of the loss. Keep that in mind for this week — Big Tech earnings, the Fed, the ECB, the BoJ, and a French budget that's still in pieces. Volatility shows up whenever it likes.
