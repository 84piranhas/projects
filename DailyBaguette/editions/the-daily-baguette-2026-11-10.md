**Europe.** It started flat to a bit lower and closed firmly higher. Lower oil helped, and so did the banks. **UniCredit** gained about 6% after a Q3 profit up 24% y/y and a higher full-year target. **Banco BPM** jumped close to 11% after **Crédit Agricole** sent a letter proposing talks on combining it with Crédit Agricole Italia. The French bank itself edged slightly lower, the usual price of being the one making the offer. Defence names also helped. **SAP** gained on a JPMorgan upgrade, while **Hochtief** fell after ACS dismissed takeover reports. Milan was the best-performing big market, helped by its banks.

**US.** This was the strongest session in a while after the worst week for the S&P since April 2025. Every sector rose, with industrials (+4.6%) on top, and the SOX index jumped about 6.9%. **AMD** rose about 12% intraday on an Oppenheimer upgrade to outperform ($600 target), and Broadcom gained around 8%. **Constellation Energy** (+10%+) beat and raised guidance, Cloudflare added nearly 10%, Tesla 5%+ on robotaxi expansion, and NRG about 9% on a $4bn sale of six gas plants. On the downside, **Ionis** lost roughly a quarter after cutting its 2026 Tryngolza sales forecast to ~$50m from $100–110m.

The odd one out: **Nvidia**, down more than 4% at the close (and about 6% in Frankfurt during the day). Its board opened an independent investigation, run by independent directors with outside counsel, after reports linking CEO Jensen Huang, through a personal relationship, to a suspected Chinese intelligence operation. The board says it has no reason to believe company secrets were compromised and that it keeps its confidence in Huang. Q3 results are due 17 November, which suddenly looks like a more interesting call.

On the wider picture, Citi downgraded US equities and global tech to neutral and upgraded Europe, citing AI-related risk (per Bloomberg, relayed by TheStreet). Political backdrop: last week's midterms delivered Democratic control of both chambers.

## The fun bit

Today the US Bureau of Labor Statistics will publish an inflation report with **no annual inflation rate**. That's not because prices stopped mattering. The government shutdown of 1 October – 12 November 2025 meant October 2025 prices were never collected, so there is no base month to compare against. A few items survive, though: gasoline, new vehicles and used cars still get their 12-month changes, so the one thing you can officially know about prices over the year is how much more your car and its tank cost. Expect at least three different "unofficial" y/y numbers on your screens by 14:31. Pick the one that suits your book; everyone else will.
