**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Friday 23 October 2026 — France now pays more than Uncle Sam, and Moody's grades the homework tonight**

Good morning,

Thursday had a bit of everything, mostly bad. Meta's capex bill set off another AI-spending scare, Brent jumped about 4% after attacks in Saudi Arabia, and French debt carried on sliding until the 10-year OAT yielded more than the 10-year Treasury. That sets up Friday: flash PMIs this morning and Moody's on France after the close. Coffee first.

*Sources disagree on the DAX level (one quotes 25,271.20), but both give −1.07%. We use the figure that fits Wednesday's close above 25,600. The Euro Stoxx 50 range reflects two press sources: −1.89% and −1.94%.*

- **Paris.** French banks took the hit from the sovereign: **BNP Paribas −4.9%**, with Société Générale and Crédit Agricole also lower. Euro-area banks overall fell 4.2%, their worst day since March. **Hermès −4.9%**: its Q3 sales growth slowed and came in slightly short of expectations, and the luxury sector followed (Kering −1.3%). The AI-capex worries reached the CAC through **Schneider Electric (−5.4%)** and **Legrand (−5.8%)**. In the mid-caps, **SMCP lost 20%** after weak Q3 sales, and **ID Logistics gained 8.9%** as its growth picked up. After the close, **Kering** reported Q3 sales of €3.345bn, down 1% like-for-like. Gucci fell 6% like-for-like, and the group confirmed it still aims to improve margins in 2026.
- **Rest of Europe.** **UCB +21.6%** after raising its 2026 growth guidance, and Nestlé +1.1% on better-than-expected organic growth. **Bankinter fell 7.6%** as higher deposit costs squeezed its margin. **Barclays fell 2.3%** on a lighter Q3 profit and a plan to cut about 5,000 jobs. Semiconductors suffered: ASML, Infineon and STMicro each fell roughly 5–6%. Healthcare (+2.1%) was the only sector that offered real cover.
- **Wall Street.** The Nasdaq had its worst day since early June. **Meta fell 7.0%**: the results were fine, but the spending plans weren't (it also announced a 2-for-1 split). **Tesla fell 3.1%** despite an earnings beat, as investors focused on 2027–28 capex. Chip-equipment names were hit hardest (Lam Research ~−9%, Applied Materials and KLA ~−6%), and **CoreWeave dropped 10%**. **Blackstone rose 8.4%** on an upbeat outlook for realizations, which explains the flat Dow. The VIX rose 14% to 19.03.

**Bonds**

- **OAT 10y: ~5.06–5.08%** (+7–8bp). It touched 5.14% intraday, the highest since July 2002. **Bund 10y: ~3.34%** (down ~7bp, as haven buying came in). The **OAT–Bund spread** closed around **174bp**, after an intraday 177.7bp, the widest since November 2011. The drivers are a stalled budget, street protests and a presidential election next year. Higher energy prices add to the pressure because they keep the ECB in hawkish mode.
- **US Treasuries rallied hard on the flight to safety. The 10y ended around 4.96% (−18bp) and the 2y around 4.65% (−17bp)**, both late-session levels. The 10y is back below 5% for the first time in nearly a month, after a 5.36% peak last week. Initial jobless claims came in at 194k against 200k expected.

**FX**

- **EUR/USD 1.1213 (−0.17%).** The euro is now carrying some French risk premium.
- **USD/JPY 156.83 (−0.8%)** and **USD/CHF 0.8206 (−0.5%)**. The usual havens were in demand.

**Other**

- **Brent (Dec) settled at $113.17, up ~4%**, and **WTI at $99.47, up 2.4%**. A drone strike on Aramco's Shaybah field and attacks near Dammam and Yanbu revived supply fears. That came on top of reports that the US and Israel plan renewed strikes on Iran.
- **Gold fell ~2.9% to ~$4,022** even as yields dropped. Rate-hike pricing and some liquidation look like the culprits. **Silver lost 5.9%.**
- **Bitcoin ~$81,200 (−3.3%)**, behaving like another risk asset.

**The "why", in three lines:** markets have stopped paying for AI capex on trust, which is the tech story. Oil above $110 and a Fed still pricing a December hike are eating into the "soft landing" story. And France, after a long build-up, is now a live sovereign-risk trade rather than a talking point.

## Today — what might happen

- **Moody's cuts France from Aa3.** The negative outlook has been in place since April, and the market has done much of the agency's work for it. Polymarket prices a Moody's or S&P downgrade by 31 October at ~86%, but on very thin volume (under $5k), so treat it as an indication only. *Our call: ~65% for a one-notch cut tonight.* A downgrade would hit Monday's open rather than today's session. If Moody's holds the rating, expect some relief in the spread, but not much.
- **French PMI below 50.** Consensus sits just above it at 50.1, and protests plus political paralysis rarely help service-sector sentiment. *Our call: ~45%.*
- **Fed.** A Reuters poll (16–20 Oct) found 94 of 100 economists expect a hold on 28 October, and all 100 expect a hike in December. CME FedWatch put December at ~83% on 20 October. Yesterday's Treasury rally has probably shaved some of that off, so watch whether the US PMIs and the Michigan inflation gauge give it back.
- **ECB (29 October).** A hold at 2.50% is the consensus (Danske preview). The question for next week is whether anyone in the Governing Council mentions French spreads publicly.
- **Weekend risk.** More French protests are planned for Saturday, and the Gulf remains unstable. *Our call:* desks trim risk into the close rather than chase the bounce.

## The fun bit

Here's one for the pub quiz. On Thursday, the **French Republic paid more to borrow for ten years (~5.08%) than the US Treasury (~4.96%)**. The OAT also printed its highest yield since **July 2002**, when the euro was a few months old as a banknote and Jean-Pierre Raffarin had just taken office at Matignon. Meanwhile, **Meta is splitting its shares 2-for-1**, so after yesterday's −7% the shares at least look cheaper. Halving the price per share is the oldest trick in the book, and halving the spread will be much harder.

Bon week-end — and do keep an eye on the Moody's headline tonight.
