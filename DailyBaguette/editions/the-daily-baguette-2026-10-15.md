**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Thursday 15 October 2026 — Hot CPI, cold chips, and Europe quietly eats its greens**

Good morning,

Wednesday's US CPI came in hot enough to put an October Fed hike back on the table, and it set off a clean split between continents. Wall Street dumped AI and semis. Europe shrugged, rotated into pharma and food, and closed higher. Today's question is whether PPI and retail sales confirm the scare or let it fade. Coffee first.

**US: inflation plus AI credit jitters.** The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 4.5%. Tech was the worst S&P sector (about −3%), while health care (+2.4%) and staples (+2.2–2.4%) led. CoreWeave dropped more than 15%: its bonds now yield above 15%, a level Bloomberg associates with distress, and that revived questions about how AI spending is financed. Dell lost about 8% after a Barclays downgrade to Equal Weight, citing valuation and weak enterprise AI. **Bank earnings missed:** Bank of America fell 4.8% on a miss in trading revenue plus a $1.2bn anti-money-laundering fine. Netflix plans to cut about 1,000 jobs (around 6% of staff) ahead of its 20 October results.

**Europe: a defensive rotation.** Pharma and food carried the indices: Danone +2.6%, L'Oréal +1.8%, Sanofi +1.5%, Roche +3.1%, AstraZeneca +3.4%, Lonza +3.4% (outlook reiterated). **Airbus** gained 2.0% to €190.22. It forecast that the global aircraft services market will double by 2045 to $422bn and said it is considering faster A350 production. **Eutelsat** led the SBF 120 with +23.3% to €1.69 after reports that Starlink service in Ukraine had been restricted or degraded. **ASML** beat on Q3 (EPS €12.95 vs €10.68 expected), guided FY sales to €44–45bn and said supply won't fully meet 2027–28 demand. Shares were up 1.7% in the session per MoneyVox, but Saxo says the early pop faded once rate-hike fears took over. Rheinmetall rose 3.1%.

### The "why", in four lines

September CPI rose 0.6% m/m (3.7% y/y vs 3.6% expected), with gasoline up 9.1%. The bigger problem was **core: +0.335% m/m vs 0.2% expected, 2.6% y/y**, driven by shelter, airfares and used cars. With the Fed already at 3.75–4.00% after September's hike, CME FedWatch odds of another hike on **28 October** roughly doubled to **about 43–50%** (Invezz and Newsquawk citing CME), and two hikes are now priced by end-January. Goolsbee called the print "discouraging" but wants more data. Barkin is waiting for PPI. Long-duration tech took the hit, defensives got the bid, and Europe looked relatively cheap and boring, which is exactly what investors wanted yesterday.

## Today — what might happen

- **PPI and retail sales decide October.** If core PPI prints at 0.4% or above alongside firm retail sales, FedWatch odds of an October hike should move clearly above 50%. Our call: about 35% that we get that combination. A soft pair would push the odds back towards the pre-CPI ~20%.
- **TSMC sets the tone for semis.** A beat on profit is the base case (consensus already implies a record), but yesterday's selling was about rates and AI financing, not demand. Our call: about 60% that a beat fails to lift the SOX meaningfully if yields rise after 14:30.
- **The defensive rotation keeps going in Europe.** Pharma and staples have momentum, and ASML/semis remain the weak spot. Our call: about 55% that the CAC outperforms the Nasdaq again today.
- **ECB:** a December hike is priced. Any hawkish ECB speaker would mostly confirm it rather than add to it. Watch the OAT–Bund spread near 140bp.
- **Oil above $105 Brent** stays headline-driven (Hormuz, Houthis). There's no scheduled catalyst except EIA inventories at 18:00.

## The fun bit

On this day in 2008, **15 October**, the Dow fell **733 points (−7.87%) to 8,577.91**, then its second-largest point drop ever. The trigger was weak retail sales, soft manufacturing data and a jump in core inflation. Eighteen years later, the morning's agenda again features US retail sales, manufacturing surveys and an inflation print the day after a hot core reading. The odd one out: oil fell more than $4 that day to $74.54, its lowest of 2008. Today Brent sits at $106. History rhymes, but it clearly checks the energy bill first.
