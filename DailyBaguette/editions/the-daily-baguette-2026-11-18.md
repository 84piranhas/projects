- **Luxury slipped:** Hermès −5.1%, Kering −3.6%, LVMH −2.7%. TotalEnergies was the only CAC 40 stock in the green (+0.02%).

- **US:** Home Depot −2.5% and Disney −1.5% despite solid numbers; JD.com −7.7%. Intel was hit hard again.
- **After the bell, Nvidia (fiscal Q3):** revenue $115.8bn vs ~$108.8bn expected; EPS $2.68 vs $2.47; data centre $107.6bn. Q4 guide: ~$130bn ±3%, gross margin 71–72%. Shares were up 4–5% after hours, having closed −0.4%. Micron (+4.6%) and Credo (+7.5%) rose in sympathy.

**Bonds**

- **US Treasuries:** latest official par yields (16 Nov) are 2y 4.83% and 10y 5.12%. The Treasury had not posted the 17 Nov row at the time of writing; the 10y traded near 5.10% intraday (Capital Street FX).
- **France/Germany 10y (indicative market levels):** OAT 4.91% (−2bp), Bund 3.35% (−3bp). That puts the **OAT-Bund spread at 156bp** by our calculation, about one basis point wider on the day. OATs held up better intraday after Marine Le Pen's pension plan calmed some fiscal nerves.
- **Supply:** Germany sold €3.8bn of 2028 Schatz at an average 3.10% (1.8x cover); the UK's 2041 gilt drew 3.28x at 5.591%.
- Fed's Musalem said several rate hikes would likely be needed by spring. CME FedWatch prices a December hike at ~95%.

**FX**

- **EUR/USD** closed at 1.0999, essentially flat (IBKR). It dipped below 1.10 intraday before recovering. USD/JPY spent the day in a 158.9–159.6 range, close enough to 160 to keep Tokyo awake.

**Other**

- **Brent +0.8% to ~$93.2**, clawing back part of Monday's ~4% drop as US-Iran tensions in the Gulf flared again.
- **Gold** went nowhere, held under $3,900. Copper fell to a four-month low, and miners slid 1.4%.

**The why, in short:** a hawkish Fed (hikes, not cuts, are the 2026 storyline), hot US import prices (+1.1% m/m) and a softer industrial production print (−0.4%) set the tone. On top of that came the Nvidia binary event, and investors chose to hit the sell button first. Retail sales were in line, with a firm control group (+0.6%). That fits "economy fine, inflation sticky" and does nothing to soften the Fed.

## The fun bit

On **18 November 1883**, US and Canadian railroads switched to standardised time zones. Until then, every town kept its own local solar time. That gave the day a nickname, "the day of two noons", because clocks in many cities were reset at midday and hit noon twice. Traders who struggle to sync the 15:30 Paris US open with their lunch break can at least be glad the mess was sorted out 143 years ago. Daylight saving time is another story.
