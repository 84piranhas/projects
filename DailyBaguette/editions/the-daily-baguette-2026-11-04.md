**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Wednesday 4 November 2026 — Blue wave, red futures**

Good morning. America voted, the chips bounced, and Apple found out what memory costs. Overnight, projections handed Democrats both chambers of Congress, and markets took it as a reason to sell Nasdaq futures and the dollar. Coffee first; then the damage.

**Europe** carried the weight of the US tech wobble. Investors are still questioning the return on AI spending, and Apple's margin warning did not help. SAP lost 1.83% and ASML 1.63%. Basic resources fell nearly 2% and retail more than 1.5%.

- **Alstom** fell another 4.8%, after losing 26.1% on Monday's profit warning. Bargain hunters remain theoretical.
- **bioMérieux** gained 4.8% on quarterly revenue, and **LVMH** rose 1.99% to €401.50.
- **Philips** gained almost 5% on results and a preliminary agreement with the US Justice Department over its respirator recall probes.
- **Bayer** added more than 4.5% after raising its 2026 profit outlook.
- **Ferrari** led Euro Stoxx 50 gainers on an earnings beat and a slightly higher EBITDA target.

**Wall Street** split in two. Chipmakers rebounded from a brutal run, with the Nasdaq-100 having closed Monday in correction territory: AMD and Broadcom rose about 3%, Nvidia and Micron more than 2%. **Apple** fell 1.90% (about $94bn of market value) on its holiday-quarter margin guidance and rising memory costs, which pulled the Dow lower. **Palantir** lost 1.65% despite 96% revenue growth and raised guidance, as new US contract growth slowed.

After the bell, **AMD** posted record Q3 revenue of $13.4bn and guided Q4 to about $17bn; the shares rose 6.5–8% after hours. **Arista** gained 11–14%. **Skyworks** dropped 17% on its outlook, and **Amdocs** was marked down about 43% on its forecast and a dispute with T-Mobile.

### The why

Tuesday's session was positioning: a bounce in beaten-up chips against Apple and rising yields. The real news came after the close. By European dawn, CBS and the AP were reported to have Democrats winning the Senate gains they need, and CBS projected a Democratic House, with the AP more cautious on that call and the majority's size unknown. These are election-night projections, not certified results.

Asia sold: the Nikkei was down 2–3% on its return from holiday and the Kospi fell 1.6%. Reuters reported S&P futures down 1.2%, Nasdaq futures down more than 2% and European futures down 1.4%. The stated fears are less fiscal stimulus and more regulation of AI and crypto. Not everyone is convinced: State Street's Michael Arone sees the larger risk as "investors overreacting to a blue wave."

## The fun bit

History's warning on election-week euphoria comes with exact change. On 4 November 2008, the Dow rose more than 300 points as Americans went to vote. Over the next two sessions it lost 929.49 points (-9.66%), closing at 8,695.79. CNN called it the biggest two-day point decline in the Dow's history at the time. Weak jobs data did the damage rather than the ballots, which is worth remembering with payrolls due on Friday.

Meanwhile, more than $1.48bn was wagered on this year's midterms on prediction markets, according to Reuters. Democracy, now with a bid-ask spread.
