**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Friday 13 November 2026 — Nvidia loses its leather jacket, Paris keeps its cool**

Good morning,

Friday the 13th, and the superstitious among you may note that the bad luck arrived a day early. Nvidia's founder walked out of the building yesterday, US 10-year yields are back above 5%, Brent is nudging $96 — and yet the CAC 40 still squeezed out another gain. Coffee first, then the details.

**The big story: Jensen Huang resigns.** In a filing to the SEC, Nvidia's board said Huang had resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO and keeps the CFO role. Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys chief, becomes the company's first-ever board chair. The release gives no reason. French and US press link the exit to reporting on his personal relationships. NVDA fell as much as ~14% early, then recovered to close **−4.34% at $166.19**. That is still about $250bn of market cap gone, by MoneyVox's count, on top of a ~20% slide in November. Citi cut its target from $285 to $225 (per WSJ, cited by Eurasia Business News). Rivals took the opposite side: **AMD +5.9%** and **Broadcom +5.5%**, as investors bet on market-share gains. The Nasdaq 100 even went green intraday, but the broad tape didn't follow. Rate-hike worries hit small caps hardest, with the Russell down 1.7%. Speculative names led the losses: Archer, Lucid, IonQ, Opendoor and Joby were all down 9–11%, plus Super Micro −11.3% and Global Payments −12%.

**Paris** dithered from 2 pm, when Donald Trump posted that the US doesn't "need a deal with Iran". It still closed higher, held up by oil and defensives. **TotalEnergies +1.6%**, and **Sanofi** and **Engie** each rose more than 1.5%. Luxury was barely positive (LVMH +0.5%, Hermès +0.1%, Kering flat). Among smaller names, **Vente-unique +8.3%** after full-year sales rose 21% to €243.1m, and **Viridien +2.2%** on an AlphaValue buy note and a Sinopec order for 80,000 seismic sensors. Autos had a weak afternoon in Tradingsat's late-session movers table: Stellantis −4.4% and Renault −3.7%.

**Frankfurt**: insurers had a good results day. **Munich Re +3.2%**, Allianz ~+1.9% and Siemens ~+2.1% all reported Q3 numbers, which Trading Economics' write-up called better than expected. Its auto-generated EPS headlines disagree, so look at the line items before trusting either. Losers: GEA −4.7%, Rheinmetall −2.8%, Fresenius Medical Care −2.7%. **Merck KGaA** lifted its full-year guidance but still fell ~1–2%. In **London**, **Burberry jumped ~7.7%** after H1 operating profit of £62m beat the ~£44m expected.

### Bonds

The precise close is n/a from the sources we could read, as is the 2-year. The 10s–2s spread is about +31 bp on the same snapshot. Drivers: a hot October CPI earlier in the week, Waller's hawkish comments and higher oil.

- **OAT–Bund spread: ~151 bp**, our calculation from those two figures. France is underperforming again, and the spread is wider than any of us would like at a dinner party.
- **BTP 10-year: ~4.39–4.40%**. That puts Italy ~45 bp *inside* France, which is still a strange sight.

- **DXY ~103.2**, near a 19-month high. TradingKey says Thursday's 103.02 was the highest since April 2025.

### Other assets

- **Brent settled +3.3% at $95.82**, and **WTI +2.6% at $88.74** (Reuters, via Eurasia Business News). Reported attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and disruption around Hormuz did more than Trump's Truth Social post.
- **Gold fell ~2.8–3.0% to ~$3,876–3,880/oz** (Kitco spot and Motley Fool). That is below $3,900, as real yields and the dollar both rose. Silver −3.1%.
- **Bitcoin** had a rough day as well. Motley Fool shows ~$68.5k (−6.8%) and TraderFactor ~$71.1k this morning. The sources disagree, so take the direction rather than the level.
- **TTF gas** rose ~2% to ~€88/MWh intraday.

### The why, in three lines

1. **The rates regime has changed.** After a hot CPI, markets now see a December Fed *hike* as a near-certainty, and long yields are back above 5%. That weighs on duration-sensitive and speculative equities.
2. **Nvidia's governance shock** was an idiosyncratic hit to the market's biggest weight. Chip rivals and Europe's value-heavy indices barely felt it, or gained.
3. **Oil.** The Gulf tensions are pushing up inflation expectations, which feeds point 1. Energy stocks in Europe absorbed the shock well.

## The fun bit

Today is the **third and last Friday the 13th of 2026**. The others were in February and March. That pair is no coincidence: in a non-leap year, February has exactly 28 days, so 13 March falls on the same weekday as 13 February. Good news for the superstitious trader: the next one isn't until **August 2027**. Bad news: PPI didn't check the calendar before choosing its release date.

A thought for Nvidia's investor relations team, who now have four days to rewrite an earnings call script built around a man best known for his leather jacket.

Have a lucky Friday, and a good weekend.
