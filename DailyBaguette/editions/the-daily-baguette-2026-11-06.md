**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Friday 6 November 2026 — The chips are down, and payrolls are next**

Good morning. Wall Street had its worst day since April 2025, Paris somehow closed in the green, and the Bank of England raised rates in the middle of it all. Asia has since tripped a circuit breaker, and the jobs report lands at 14:30. Coffee first.

**The US.** The Philadelphia semiconductor index lost more than 12%. Micron fell 17.5%, AMD 17.3%, Marvell 16.4%, Broadcom 10.4% and Nvidia 9.3%. Tesla shed 10.8%. The Nasdaq now sits about 12% below its October record, which is correction territory. The VIX closed at 28.18, up about 70%, and only 71 S&P members rose.

**Earnings.** Qualcomm (-9.5%) missed on phone chips and guided light. Arm fell 13.1% on results the market read as mixed. AppLovin (+31.0%) beat and guided strongly. After hours, Cloudflare and Airbnb rose, while Atlassian and DraftKings fell by double digits.

**Europe.** Air France-KLM (+12.1%) flew on a Q3 operating profit about 26% above forecasts. JCDecaux gained 5.8% and Bouygues 2.6%, the latter after raising its profitability outlook. Soitec (-16.7%) and STMicro (-9.3%) took the AI hit. Legrand gave up a near-6% morning gain to close -0.1%, despite raising guidance and close to 40% organic growth in data centres. Rheinmetall fell sharply after cutting its 2026 sales forecast for the second time in three months.

### Bonds

- **Treasuries:** the 10-year ended near 4.84% (about -6bp) and the 2-year near 4.46% (about -12bp), so the curve steepened to about 38bp.
- **Bund:** 10-year about 3.25% (-4bp), the lowest since late August.
- **OAT:** 10-year about 4.78%, flat, so French paper missed the rally. That puts the spread near 154bp on our arithmetic. Treat the level with care: another source showed 156bp on Wednesday with quite different yields.
- **Gilts:** 10-year around 5.1%.

### FX

EUR/USD ended around 1.122 (sources range from 1.1222 to 1.1230 depending on the time of day). GBP/USD was about 1.345 and USD/JPY about 157.7, with the yen a little firmer. For a 4% equity rout, FX was remarkably calm.

### Other

- **Brent:** $98.29, up about 3.8%, recovering part of Wednesday's 8.7% slide on renewed Iran supply worries. WTI closed near $90.50.
- **Gold:** roughly $4,090-4,100, up about 0.5%. It is near $4,045 this morning.
- **Crypto:** bitcoin about $75,900 (-5.7% over 24 hours), ether about $2,235 (-7.8%).

### Why it happened

The Democratic midterm sweep was first read as good news. Thursday's reading was that it threatens the data-centre build-out, with Reuters reporting local opposition in key states and UBS estimating about 30% of the capacity additions expected for 2028 at risk. Add disappointing chip earnings and crowded positioning, and the exit got narrow.

Paris escaped largely because of its low tech weight and a good earnings day, but also because of timing. Europe closed with the Nasdaq about -4%, and it finished at -5.5%. Some of that bill may be presented this morning.

The macro backdrop was no comfort to bond bulls. Jobless claims were 189,000, the lowest in 57 years according to Trading Economics, and Q3 productivity rose 3.6% against 2.8% expected.

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate 25bp to 4% on an 8-1 vote, with Catherine Mann wanting 50bp. It sees UK CPI at around 5% by mid-2027. The Fed is priced to follow: traders see a December *hike* as more likely than not, though the odds slipped from about 83% to about 70%.

## Today — what might happen

- **December Fed hike:** priced at 68-70% (CME FedWatch via CNBC and Trading Economics). A strong jobs print would push this back up, awkwardly for equities.
- **Payrolls above +65k:** our call ~55%. ADP at +86k and very low claims argue for it, but the bar has been lowered by recent weak prints.
- **Unemployment at or below 4.2%:** our call ~70%.
- **CAC 40 opens lower:** our call ~75%, on catch-up with the US close and Asia.
- **Nasdaq Composite closes higher:** our call ~55%. Bounces after days like this are common, but they are not a floor. A good payrolls number could help earnings hopes and hurt rate hopes at the same time.
- **Brent settles above $100:** our call ~35%. It remains on course for a weekly loss above 11%.

## The fun bit

Thursday's market verdict on the future of humanity: doughnuts beat memory chips. Krispy Kreme, which raised its 2026 revenue forecast, closed up about 31%, according to StockTitan's table of closing movers. Micron lost 17.5% the same day. One portfolio bet on artificial intelligence, the other on artificial glaze, and only one of them needed a data-centre permit.

Patriotic footnote: on the worst day for US stocks in 18 months, the best large story in Paris was an airline. We make no claim that this is a sustainable investment thesis.

Have a good Friday, and mind the 14:30 step.
