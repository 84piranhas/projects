**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Monday 2 November 2026 — Intel craters, Hormuz flickers, Ternus takes the mic**

Good morning,

Friday was a bad day to own anything with a transistor in it. Intel's guidance took the Nasdaq down nearly 3% and closed a poor month on a poor note, while Paris finished flat thanks to Sanofi and a helping of luxury. Over the weekend, talks in Doha knocked more than 3% off crude. This week brings Apple tonight, the midterms and the RBA tomorrow, euro area inflation on Wednesday, the BoE on Thursday and payrolls on Friday. Do take your vitamins.

A housekeeping note: US clocks changed on Sunday, so New York now opens at 15:30 Paris time.

### The "why"

1. **AI's bill came due.** Thursday it was Alphabet, Friday it was Intel and Amazon. Investors now want free cash flow, and the Philadelphia semis index was down about 8% near the European close.
2. **Europe's data split.** France's Q3 GDP fell 0.1% (+0.1% expected), while French CPI rose to 3.4% (3.2% expected; 3.1% prior). German inflation slowed to 2.8% from 3.3%, German GDP rose 0.2% and euro area GDP rose 0.4%. Euro area unemployment stayed at 6.5%.
3. **Oil swung both ways.** Friday's rally came on talk of renewed US strikes and tanker incidents in Hormuz. Then the mood reversed: Iranian negotiators are in Qatar, Iran says a Qatari plan could reopen the strait within two days, and Trump is reported to be giving diplomacy more time. OPEC+ held December targets for a third month.
4. **China's PMIs disagree.** The official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 (from 50.1), the private RatingDog gauge hit 52.0. The PBOC also launched a daily overnight reverse repo at 1.25%.

## The fun bit

On **2 November 1947**, Howard Hughes took the Spruce Goose for its one and only flight: a wooden aircraft with a 320-foot wingspan and a $23m price tag. It flew briefly, proved a point and never carried a passenger.

Amazon's Q3 capex alone was reportedly about $77bn. In unadjusted dollars, that's roughly 3,300 Spruce Geese in a single quarter. Inflation would shrink that count considerably, but the market's question is the same one asked of Hughes: lovely machine, but when does it pay?

— **The Daily Baguette**
