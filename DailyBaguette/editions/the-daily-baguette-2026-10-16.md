**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Friday 16 October 2026 — Chips down, lipstick up, yields off the boil**

Good morning,

Thursday was a lesson in how two scorching US inflation prints can still produce a bond rally. Producer prices blew through forecasts, yet Treasuries and Bunds rallied hard, the Dow added 1%, and Paris rode luxury and consumer names while anything with "AI" or "data centre" in the investor deck got sold. Warsh spoke overnight in Bangkok and, as advertised, said nothing about October. Coffee first, then the numbers.

**Paris beat the eurozone.** The CAC 40 gained 0.78% while the Euro Stoxx 50 lost 0.39%. The gap comes down to who owns the chipmakers. Hermès (+3.3%) and L'Oréal (+3.2%) carried Paris; Carrefour (+2.5%, UBS) and JCDecaux (+3.9%, Deutsche Bank upgrade) helped. On the other side sat the AI-infrastructure crowd: STMicro −3.8%, Schneider Electric −2.4%, Legrand −1.2%, Soitec −7.7%. Infineon fell about 5% and was the worst stock in the DAX. Pernod Ricard edged up 0.8% after a Q1 sales drop that was smaller than feared, with 2026-27 organic guidance kept. Ericsson jumped 6.8% on a Q3 beat. Off-index, **GL Events rose 28%**, its best session ever, after strong Q3 growth and higher annual guidance.

**Wall Street rotated rather than retreated.** The S&P was down 0.6% early, then recovered. Roughly seven in ten S&P members closed higher. Health care (+1.9%, a record high for XLV), materials and consumer discretionary led; tech (−0.65%) and energy (−1.6%) lagged. Homebuilders liked the drop in yields. Nvidia lost 1.8%. **CoreWeave** shows how nervous the AI-credit trade has become: its 5-year CDS hit a record and the stock was down 9.5% intraday, then it closed **+5.9%** after Jensen Huang said its cost of capital was falling. TSMC posted a record profit (+68.7% y/y) and guided Q4 above consensus, but its gross margin slipped to 66.4% and the ADR traded lower. Bank earnings were mixed. BNY and PNC beat on EPS, but PNC's revenue fell slightly short. U.S. Bancorp raised its FY26 revenue outlook, and Schwab brought in record net new assets but just missed on revenue. Marsh & McLennan rose 4.5% after 10% adjusted EPS growth.

### The why, in four sentences

US September PPI rose **1.3% m/m and 6.3% y/y**, against roughly 0.5–0.6% and 5.5% expected, its largest gain since 2022. Energy did most of the work, with gasoline up 20%, and core ex-food, energy and trade came in at a calmer 0.5% m/m. Retail sales printed **+0.8%** (vs +0.3%), but the control group managed just +0.1%, which helped the bond rally. Investors read the mix as an energy shock rather than a demand boom, bought duration, and left AI names that borrow heavily to fund data centres, because those suffer most when rates stay high. French CPI fell 0.3% m/m in September, but the annual rate is still 3.1%.

**Overnight and futures (as of the Asian session):** Warsh refused to "prejudge" the 27–28 October FOMC. He said the September hike had "removed a dose of accommodation", called October and December "crystallising moments", and spoke well of AI's economic impact. Cleveland's Hammack sounded more hawkish, warning that an inflationary mindset could force tougher action. Asia bought the dip: MSCI Asia-Pacific +1.4%, Korea +3.4%, Topix +1.8%, Hang Seng +1.3%. **S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures were +0.2% and Euro Stoxx 50 futures +0.8%** at the last check, and the US 10y was steady around 5.07%. On geopolitics, Trump told Newsmax he would not use nuclear weapons or send ground troops into Iran, but did not rule out a strike on a site near Natanz.

## Today — what might happen

- **Fed hike on 28 October:** CME FedWatch put the odds at **~29%** (70.7% hold) on Thursday morning after the PPI. Bloomberg's ~42% reading on Wednesday came before yesterday's bond rally. Warsh's non-answer changes nothing. *Our call: odds stay in the 25–35% range today unless import prices badly surprise.*
- **Europe catches up at the open:** given the Euro Stoxx futures and Asia's rebound, *our call: ~70% the Euro Stoxx 50 closes higher*, with semis (ASML, Infineon, STMicro) leading if Treasury yields hold below 5.10%.
- **10y Treasury back above 5.19%** (a technical level some strategists are watching) today: *our call: ~15%*. A stronger IP print alone is unlikely to do it; an oil spike might.
- **BoE (5 Nov) and ECB:** markets price a high chance of a BoE hike. On the ECB, Lithuania's Šimkus said a move "as early as this month" is still possible, but Lagarde sounded more cautious. Expect more central bankers to speak from Bangkok; any hawkish ECB comment would hit OATs harder than Bunds.
- **Oil headline risk:** reports of drone/missile activity near the Strait of Hormuz are still unverified. *Our call: Brent's $100 floor is more likely to hold than to break today (~75%).*

## The fun bit

**On this day, 39 years ago — Friday 16 October 1987 —** the Dow fell **108.35 points (−4.6%)**. That came on a Friday, ahead of the following Monday. London never traded that day: the Great Storm had blown through overnight and the exchange stayed shut. When it reopened on Monday, the FTSE was down 14% by midday. So today: Friday, options expiry, a hawkish Fed and stretched AI valuations. We are not predicting anything, but you may want to keep an umbrella by the desk.

Bonne journée, and mind the strikes.
