**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Monday 9 November 2026 — Chips bounce, the Fed sharpens its pencil, oil has its worst week since 2020**

Good morning,

Last week will be remembered for one ugly Thursday: Wall Street's AI-and-midterms tantrum (Nasdaq −5.5%, its worst day since April 2025) wiped out a lot of conviction in a single session. Friday brought a partial chip rebound in New York, but Europe mostly caught the tail of Thursday's hangover. Then a blowout US payrolls print pushed a December *hike* firmly into the base case. Futures are greener this morning. Coffee first, conviction second.

**Paris** slipped back after being down as much as 1.9% intraday; 35 of the 40 members ended in the red. Luxury and tech did the damage: LVMH −2.4%, Hermès −1.8%, Kering −1.8% (Pierpaolo Piccioli is leaving Balenciaga), Capgemini −2.8%. Euronext fell 2.5% despite record Q3 results and a €350m buyback — "sell the good news" weather. Air France-KLM added another 7.1% after Thursday's 12% results pop, helped by a two-notch Barclays upgrade. STMicro clawed back 1.5% after losing 14% in three sessions. Off-index, Teleperformance sank 15.3% after Q3 revenue slipped 1.2% and 2026 guidance was cut to a 1.5–2.5% decline — the AI-disruption trade is still brutal for call-centre stocks. Nexans gained 4.4% on a 420 km Greek high-voltage cable contract.

**New York** split by style. Chips bounced (AMD +11%, Intel +7.7%, Micron +5.1%, Lam +8.2% on a $25bn buyback, Nvidia +2.8%) and recovered roughly a third of Thursday's losses on average, while defensives lagged (staples −1.3%). Earnings were the story: Airbnb +11.7% (beat, raised growth view, up to $12bn buyback), Cloudflare +9.9% (fastest growth in almost four years), IonQ +23% (guidance well above consensus), Moderna +5.3% (2026 revenue growth guide lifted to 15–25%). On the losing side: DraftKings −21% (wider loss, spending on its predictions product), Axon −18%, Atlassian −16% (data-centre revenue outlook), Microchip −10% (soft guidance). The S&P still posted its worst week since the April 2025 tariff shock.

**Why it moved:** Thursday's rout was pinned on the Democrats' midterm sweep of both chambers, with markets fearing tighter rules on AI and data centres (many winners ran against data-centre build-outs). Friday's bounce was partly technical, partly helped by President Trump saying he fully backs the AI build-out. Europe, closing earlier, mostly priced Thursday rather than Friday.

**Weekend:** Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang over an alleged relationship with a suspected Chinese spy — one to watch at the open. In Paris, the National Assembly unanimously rejected the revenue part of the 2027 budget bill; the text goes back to the Senate in its original version.

With no top-tier US data today, Fed-speak and the 3-year auction set the tone. *Our call:* the front end consolidates; Tuesday's CPI decides whether 77% becomes 90% or 55%.
- **Tech rebound, part two?** Futures argue yes, but the Nvidia governance probe is a fresh, idiosyncratic risk. *Our call: ~60%* that the Nasdaq-100 closes higher today, with Nvidia underperforming the chip complex.
- **Oil:** headline-driven around the Qatar meeting. Qatar insists talks are alive, while Rubio called them "not productive". *Our call:* Brent stays in a $95–101 range today, with downside skew if a ceasefire framework emerges.

## The fun bit

Thirty-seven years ago today, on 9 November 1989, the Berlin Wall opened — reportedly after a Politburo spokesman fumbled a press-conference answer and said the new travel rules applied "immediately". Lesson for anyone running a central bank this December: forward guidance is a precision instrument. Handle with care, and maybe read the briefing note *before* the microphone goes on.

Also noted: Brent's 16% weekly slide, its worst since April 2020, came in a week when the Strait of Hormuz was still disrupted. The market prices peace talks faster than tankers can move.
