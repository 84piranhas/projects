Good morning,

**Europe — bruised, not broken.** The CAC was off more than 1% in early afternoon before trimming losses as Brent rolled over late in the day. The pressure came from two directions: French sovereign stress (see Bonds) and another round of "will AI ever pay for itself?" doubts that hit chips and electrification names.

- **Schneider Electric** fell 3–4% after launching a €6bn capital increase at €240 a share, partly to fund its PTC acquisition — priced about 21% below the pre-deal level. Shareholders were thrilled, as you'd imagine.
- AI-adjacent industrials and semis lagged: **Rexel** -4.1%, **Mersen** -4.05%, **Legrand** -3.05%, **Soitec** -3.7%; across Europe, **ABB** lost ~5.7% intraday despite record orders, and **ASML**, **Infineon** and **BESI** shed roughly 2.5–3%.
- **Solvay** dropped ~5.5% intraday after cutting its annual outlook.
- On the bright side: **OVHcloud** jumped ~18% on full-year results and a 2027 adjusted EBITDA margin target above 42.5%; **Virbac** +2.4% on a strong Q3.
- **After the close: Michelin** posted Q3 growth of 2.2% at constant FX (0.8% like-for-like), with volumes still soft (-0.8% over nine months, OE weakness) and 2026 guidance confirmed. Zonebourse showed the stock indicated ~4.5% lower in after-hours quotes — one to watch at the open.

**US — the Dow did the heavy lifting.** A broad, value-tilted session: the equal-weight S&P beat the cap-weighted index, small caps led, and megacap tech sat on its hands ahead of tonight's reports. Health care hit a record high (XLV +2.2% intraday), helped by lower yields after weak housing data.

- **General Motors** -7.5%, its worst day since July 2025, despite a Q3 beat (adj. EPS $3.70, revenue $50bn) and a $20bn buyback; full-year EPS guidance of $12.75–13.75 straddled a $13.41 consensus midpoint, and that was enough to disappoint.
- **3M** rose ~4–5% on results; **Northrop Grumman** gained after adj. EPS of $7.89 vs $7.18 expected; **Hasbro** +5.8% on a revenue beat and raised forecast.
- **Equifax** +11% and **TransUnion** +7% on credit-bureau results despite Equifax trimming its revenue guidance.
- **Salesforce** agreed to buy **Braze** for ~$5.66bn ($47.50/share cash); Braze jumped more than 50%.
- **After hours:** **Netflix** fell ~5.6% — revenue +11.8% to $12.87bn, but Q4 guide of $13.44bn missed $13.51bn and the 2026 ad-revenue forecast was cut to ~$2.7bn from ~$3bn. **SpaceX** rose on a 161% revenue surge to $14.58bn (AI revenue now about half the total). **Super Micro** jumped 14%+ after raising preliminary fiscal Q1 revenue to $18.5–19.5bn.

**The French story.** Investors have been trimming French paper for weeks; weekend violence on 17–18 October added a social-unrest layer on top of fiscal worries, and Monday–Tuesday saw the spread blow out from 137 bp to above 150 bp (ING puts the session peak at 153–154 bp). The early-October widest, 158.7 bp, was the worst since November 2011. Adding insult: the National Assembly **postponed the vote on the revenue part of the 2027 budget** to 27 October, with 2,000+ amendments still to process and a 5% deficit target the PM himself reportedly concedes may be missed. The one mitigating detail, flagged by Neuberger: ~92% of the 2026 OAT issuance programme is already done, so supply isn't the problem — demand is.

**US curve:** a bull steepener at the long end — weak September housing starts (1.221m vs 1.305m expected) and a 7.3% drop in pending home sales pulled 10s and 30s lower, while 2s edged up on a Fed that markets still expect to hike in December.

## The fun bit

This week marks the 39th anniversary of Black Monday: on 19 October 1987, the Dow lost **508 points — 22.6%** — in a single session, still its worst day in percentage terms. Yesterday, the Dow **gained 530 points**. In 1987 that would have been a crash in reverse; on Tuesday it was a modest +1.02% that most people missed because they were refreshing the OAT screen. Inflation, as ever, is the ultimate index-points magician.

Bonus Paris irony: Michelin shares and Michelin stars both reward consistency — but only the guide hands out a star for "worth a detour." The tyre maker is still waiting for volumes to make the trip.
