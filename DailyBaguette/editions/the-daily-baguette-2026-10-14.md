**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Wednesday 14 October 2026 — Hot core, cold feet: the Fed hike is back on the menu**

Good evening, and apologies for the late bake. This edition was put together at about 19:45 Paris time, so "yesterday" is Tuesday's session, and the parts of today's calendar that have already printed are given as actual results. Short version: Tuesday was flat, with banks and memory chips doing the moving. Wednesday then brought a core CPI hot enough to put an October Fed hike back at roughly even odds.

*Cross-checks: the CAC (Zonebourse and Boursorama) and the US indices (TheStreet and Investrade) agree. The DAX figure is from finanz.net via ad-hoc-news. For the Euro Stoxx 50 we found only one source.*

- **Europe:** Cheaper oil and lower yields lifted stocks in the morning. Mixed US bank results erased those gains in the afternoon; the DAX had traded briefly above 25,400 before turning lower. **LVMH** fell 1.2% on Q3 organic growth of only +1%, with fashion & leather down 5%. Chips were weak again on reports that China's CXMT has moved forward in memory: **ASML** −1.8%, **Infineon** −2.1%, and **STMicro** also lower. **Eramet** dropped 14.4% after confirming a €500m capital increase that would make the French state its largest shareholder. **Publicis** rose 2.2% on a Q3 net income beat. In Frankfurt, **Symrise** (+3.1%) and **BMW** (+2.6%) led, while **Siemens Energy** (−2.5%) lagged.
- **US banks, the first results of the season:**
  - **Goldman Sachs:** EPS $17.01 against $13.02 expected. Equities revenue was up 72% at $6.44bn.
  - **JPMorgan:** EPS $5.57 against $5.90 expected, hurt by $658m of securities losses. Revenue was $50.2bn. The shares still finished about 1% higher after the bank raised its NII guidance.
  - **Citi:** beat.
  - **Wells Fargo:** EPS beat, but NII and revenue missed. The CFO said trading slowed sharply in late September.
- **Other US movers:**
  - **UnitedHealth** rallied after beating on Q3 (adj. EPS $4.69 vs $4.06) and raising its FY26 guidance to at least $20.50.
  - **J&J** fell 5.3%. MedTech missed, and net profit was down 96% because of a talc-related charge.
  - **Micron, Western Digital and SanDisk** sold off on the same CXMT news.
  - **Six Flags** dropped 14.5% after a fatal accident on a ride in Georgia.
  - **Bumble** jumped on a $6.05-per-share take-private offer.

### Bonds: a quieter day after a rough quarter

- **US 10-year Treasury:** about 5.22%, down roughly 2bp, as haven demand returned after the Columbus Day holiday. The Fed's H.15 table for 13 Oct was not yet published. The latest official reading is 5.24% on 9 Oct.
- **US 2-year:** about 4.78% early on Wednesday (n/a for Tuesday's close).
- **Bund 10-year:** 3.44%, down from 3.47%.
- **OAT 10-year:** about 4.84%. Sources range from 4.82% to 4.85%; Banque de France data for the day was not yet available.
- **OAT–Bund spread:** about 140bp, our calculation from the figures above. It was 139.5bp on 12 Oct. Paris is still paying a hefty premium over Berlin.

### FX

- **EUR/USD:** about 1.120. The euro recovered above 1.12 as the DXY fell 0.4% to 102.2, pulling back from 18-month highs. The ECB fixing on Wednesday morning was 1.1195.
- **USD/JPY:** about 158.4 early on Wednesday. The yen remains a slow-motion story.

### Other assets

- **Brent:** down about 1–2%, to between $104.6 and $105.7 depending on the time of the print. Prices fell after Washington temporarily eased restrictions on Russian diesel. Refiners rallied on wide crack spreads.
- **Gold (December contract):** $4,177, down 0.3%.
- **Bitcoin:** Tuesday close n/a. It stayed in a tight range, then broke down on Wednesday (see below).

**Why it moved.** Two forces offset each other on the indices. Lower oil and lower yields helped rate-sensitive sectors and real estate. Chips and the weaker bank results pushed the other way. Underneath the flat close, the market was waiting for CPI with a 3.75–4.00% fed funds rate and a Fed that raised rates in September.

## Today — what might happen

- **An October Fed hike (27–28 Oct):** CME FedWatch put the probability at **43.7% after CPI, up from 22.1% before**. Other cuts of the same data show around 49%. Prediction markets: Polymarket at 43%. In other words, a coin toss.
- **Beige Book tone:** if the regional reports describe sticky prices and resilient activity, the hawkish repricing should hold. *Our call: ~60% that the Beige Book reads hawkish rather than neutral.*
- **US close:** *our call: ~75% that the S&P 500 ends the day down more than 0.5%.* Core CPI at 0.3% m/m and a 10-year moving toward 5.3% leave little room for a late rally in tech.
- **Europe tomorrow morning:** CAC futures were slightly lower after the cash close (−0.18% at 18:13). *Our call: ~65% that the CAC opens lower on Thursday* and gives back part of today's ASML-driven gains.
- **OAT–Bund spread:** with US yields rising, *our call: ~55% that the spread widens again* from about 140bp.

## The fun bit

A question for bond desks: what links 1987 and Q3 2026? In the three months to early October, the French 10-year yield rose **1.13 percentage points**, the biggest quarterly increase since **1987**. On 1 October it touched 4.96%, its highest level since July 2002.

The other famous 1987 event in markets has its anniversary next Monday (19 October). We are not suggesting the two are connected, but if a client asks why you are wearing a tie on Monday, you now have an answer.

Runner-up: Eutelsat. Today it gained 23% because of complaints about a competitor's service, not because of anything it did itself.
