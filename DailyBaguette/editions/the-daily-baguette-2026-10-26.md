**Paris was a story of individual stocks.**
- **Kering −16.6%**: Gucci sales fell 6% and directly operated stores fell 9%, with China still weak. This was the worst day in the index by some way.
- **Safran −10%**: Q3 activity came with guidance that was only *confirmed*. At these valuations the market wanted more, and that was punished.
- **Valeo −11.5%** after disappointing sales.
- **L'Oréal +5.6%**: Q3 revenue growth of 7.2% easily beat the ~5.4% expected. Lipstick is holding up better than handbags.
- **Sodexo +6.3%** on full-year results.

**Elsewhere in Europe:** Volvo Car fell ~26% to a record low as China revenue roughly halved year on year. Eni (+3.3%) led the Euro Stoxx 50 on oil. Deutsche Telekom (−3.5%) was the laggard, as telecoms took a hit from SpaceX (see below).

**Wall Street:** Selling was broad, and energy was the exception. The energy sector rose 2.4% to a record. SLB gained 6.7% on a Q3 beat.
- **Telecoms:** AT&T and T-Mobile each fell more than 6% after reports that SpaceX wants tens of thousands of cell sites for a possible fourth US carrier. Tower owners rallied, with Crown Castle up ~10–12%.
- **American Express −3.7% to −3.9%**: it raised guidance but also announced 5% job cuts.
- **Chips** steadied after Thursday's rout (SOX +0.6%).

### Bonds
- **US Treasuries:** the 10-year closed around **5.00%** (+5bp on the day, −9bp on the week) and the 2-year at **4.69%** (+5bp). Higher oil revived inflation worries.
- **France:** the OAT 10-year closed at **~5.03–5.04%**. It traded at 5.14% earlier in the week, its highest since 2002, and above Italy's yield.
- **Germany:** the Bund 10-year was at **~3.36%**.
- **OAT–Bund spread:** **~168bp**, after peaking above 170–175bp this week, its widest since 2011. OATs were the relative outperformer on Friday, as the market had already priced in a downgrade.
- **After the close, Moody's affirmed France at Aa3 with a negative outlook.** The affirmation assumes a deficit-reducing 2027 budget is passed (target 5% of GDP versus 5.4% this year). Moody's also named parliamentary fragmentation as the swing factor for its next move. This is relief, but on borrowed time.

### Other
- **Brent (Dec)** settled at **$116.72** (+3.1%), its highest settlement since late April, for a weekly gain of ~6%. **WTI** settled at $103.27 (+3.8%). Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia drove both, even as the US authorised a 400k-barrel SPR release to deal with an outage at a Louisiana refinery.
- **Gold** fell ~1.3% to ~$4,008/oz, down ~4% on the week. It briefly dropped below $4,000 as higher real yields bit.
- **Bitcoin** was roughly flat near $81k on Friday and had slipped to ~$79.5k by Sunday evening.

### The "why"
1. **Oil has replaced AI as the market's main fear.** A Brent price above $115 brings back the inflation story, which pushes yields towards 5% and lifts the odds of a December Fed hike. Long-duration assets do not like that.
2. **France remains the eurozone's problem child.** Riots, a fragile budget and a 10-year yield above 5% have cost the CAC 2.2% in a week. Moody's has bought time, not changed the trend.
3. **The AI trade is being questioned, not abandoned.** Friday's stabilisation in chips was real, but this week's hyperscaler capex guidance will decide the next leg.

### Weekend headlines
- **Brazil:** Lula won the runoff with **51.8% against Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.2%**, a margin of about 4.4m votes, after trailing in the first round. Markets had been trading a Bolsonaro win. A Tokyo-listed Bovespa ETF fell nearly 15% overnight.
- **Middle East:** the Houthis claimed new strikes on Saudi airport and oil facilities on Saturday, and Riyadh reported more interceptions on Sunday. Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials are reportedly due in Tehran today (Reuters).
- **Clocks:** Europe moved back an hour on Sunday, but the US only switches on 1 November.

## The fun bit

**On this day in 1987**, the Hong Kong exchange reopened after shutting for the rest of the week that followed Black Monday. A four-day suspension was meant to calm nerves. Instead, the Hang Seng **fell 1,120 points, or 33%, to 2,242** (SFC). The lesson: closing the market does not remove the sellers, it just makes them wait.

**A more practical one for this week:** Europe changed its clocks on Sunday and the US does not until 1 November. For five days the transatlantic gap is **five hours instead of six**. US data now hits at 13:30 Paris rather than 14:30, and the Fed statement lands at **19:00** on Wednesday. Set your alarms before Chair Warsh sets the tone.

Have a good week, and mind the gap.
