**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Tuesday 17 November 2026 — Nasdaq trips over its own CEO, all eyes on Nvidia tonight**

Good morning,

Wall Street had a Monday to forget: chips were dumped, the Nasdaq shed 2.5% and the VIX woke up. Europe got away with a scratch, cushioned by healthcare, luxury and defence. Tonight Nvidia reports for the first time without Jensen Huang at the helm, the day after the market voted with its feet. Coffee strong, please.

**Europe.** The CAC 40 opened higher, flirted with 8,400, then slid all afternoon and closed on its low, snapping a five-session winning streak. Tech did the damage: STMicro (-2.1%), Soitec (-3.3%), ASML (-2.4%), Infineon and Aixtron all lost ground as the AI trade wobbled ahead of Nvidia. Airbus (-1.2%) was also lower after reports that the bill for a French maritime patrol aircraft programme has jumped by about 65% (EUR 2.76bn to EUR 4.57bn), putting its planned end-2026 launch in doubt. On the other side, defence held firm (Thales +1.1%, Safran +1.1%), and healthcare was the best sector in Europe (+0.8%), with Novo Nordisk up 2.4% on encouraging CagriSema data. Luxury held up too (Hermès +0.6%, Richemont +2.7%). Basic resources fell about 2.5% as metals sank. FLSmidth lost 8.9% after its CFO left with immediate effect. Carmila (-2%) announced it will buy 40.9% of Italy's IGD.

**United States.** A broad selloff, led by semiconductors. Nvidia (-4.1%) is still digesting last week's news that Jensen Huang has stepped down as CEO, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO; the stock is now roughly 10% below its pre-announcement level. A new Commerce Department rule on remote access to advanced chips through overseas data centres added to the pressure. Micron (-7.6%), Intel (-7.0%), AMD (-5.6%) and Dell (-7.0%) led the decline, and big banks gave up more than 2%. Consumer staples were the only S&P 500 sector in the green. The VIX rose about 19% to 22.9. On the data side, the New York Fed's Empire State index fell 18 points to 7.8 in November, well below the 16.7 consensus.

**Earnings and single names.** Tyson Foods jumped 8.7% even though Q4 adjusted EPS fell 47% and FY2027 sales guidance was lower: the bar was clearly on the floor. Telus soared 12.4% on an unsolicited, non-binding buyout approach from Québecor. Summit Therapeutics fell 16.5% after an FDA complete response letter on ivonescimab. iQIYI dropped 17% after its Q3 results.

### Bonds

- **US Treasuries:** the official par yield curve for Monday has not been published yet. Friday's official closes were 4.88% (2-year) and 5.15% (10-year), with the 30-year at 5.47%. Market quotes on Monday evening put the 10-year around 5.12–5.15%, broadly flat after last week's inflation-driven selloff.
- **Europe (indicative market levels):** OAT 10-year around 4.94%, Bund 10-year around 3.38%, so the OAT-Bund spread is about 156 bp (our calculation). French risk is still being paid for in full.

### FX

- **EUR/USD:** ECB reference rate 1.1014 on Monday, down from 1.1052 on Friday. The euro is drifting towards the 1.10 handle as US rate-hike pricing firms up.

### Other assets

- **Brent:** around $93.5 late in the European session (-2.1%), and around $91.6 by late Monday New York time. That is a far cry from the $107 seen a month ago.
- **Gold:** around $3,850–3,865/oz, slightly lower on the day (sources differ on the exact print). Silver fell about 3% intraday.
- **Bitcoin:** around $64–65k, broadly flat on the day but down about 22% in a month. Crypto-linked stocks (Strategy, Coinbase) were each down more than 6% in early trading.

### Why it moved

Three things hit together. The market is repricing Nvidia's leadership gap two days before (now hours before) its results. Washington tightened chip-export loopholes. And with 10-year Treasuries above 5% and a December Fed hike now largely priced, the debt-funded AI capex story is getting more scrutiny: a hawkish note on corporate debt from Fed Governor Bowman did not help. Europe, with less AI and more pharma and handbags in its indices, held up better.

## Today — what might happen

- **Fed hike in December:** markets put the odds of a 25 bp hike on 9 December at about 91% (CME FedWatch as reported by Zonebourse). A strong retail sales print would make that close to certain. A weak one would revive the "Fed mistake" debate, but it would not change the December call on its own.
- **Nvidia beats on revenue:** our call: ~75%. It has a long record of beating. The real risk is not the quarter but the outlook and the tone from an interim CEO. A beat with soft guidance would be the worst mix for the Nasdaq, which already discounted a lot yesterday.
- **Home Depot moves more than 5% either way:** that is what options price. Its last four post-earnings moves averaged under 2%. Our call: a move under 5% is more likely (~65%).
- **European open:** our call: a hesitant start, slightly higher. Tech-heavy US futures are rebounding, but nobody wants to be a hero before Nvidia's numbers.

## The fun bit

On 17 November 1869, exactly 157 years ago, the Suez Canal opened, cutting the sea route from Europe to Asia by thousands of miles. The project was led by a Frenchman, Ferdinand de Lesseps, and financed largely by French savers through shares sold to the public. It was arguably Paris's first great retail-investor frenzy. In 1875, a cash-strapped Egypt sold its stake to the British government. Today, with Anthropic's order book reportedly six times covered, the lesson still holds: the people who build the channel are not always the ones who collect the tolls.
