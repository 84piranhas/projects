**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Monday 19 October 2026 — Chips bruised, Paris burning, Brent above $100**

Good morning,

A tale of two continents on Friday: Europe shrugged off the AI wobble and closed its best week since August, while Wall Street spent the afternoon shredding semiconductor exposure. Then the weekend happened — violent protests across France, another tanker reportedly hitting a mine in Hormuz, and a surprise Russia sanctions waiver from the White House. Coffee first, then the details.

**FX**

- **EUR/USD ~1.1246** at the European close (+0.3%). It eased to **1.1212–1.1225** in early Asia on the French headlines and sits close to last week's 16-month low of 1.1113. The dollar index was ~101.8, USD/JPY ~158.

**Other**

- **Brent**: up ~3.5% to ~$109.5 by the US close (it was only $106 when Paris shut), about +8% on the week, on attacks on shipping and Saudi energy sites. On the Sunday-night reopen it fell ~1.6% to **~$108** after President Trump moved to waive some Russia energy sanctions (he later said no final decision had been made). WTI ~$100.7.
- **Gold** ~$4,180–4,190/oz (+0.3–0.5%). **Bitcoin** ~$81.5k on Friday and ~$82k this morning; ether +3%.

**The why, in three sentences.** US inflation is running hot (headline CPI 3.7%), so the debate has moved from cuts to *hikes*, and 5% Treasuries are pushing up funding costs for heavily indebted AI-infrastructure builders. That has turned "AI capex" from a growth story into a credit story. Europe is less exposed to that trade and more exposed to defence and energy, so it did fine — at least until France reminded everyone of its own risk premium this weekend.

**Weekend headlines:** Saturday's cost-of-living protests drew ~550,000 people nationwide (Interior Ministry), with 1,200+ arrests and 150 police injured, and President Macron called the violence "shocking". The 2027 budget (deficit target 5.0% of GDP) is now in the Assembly with no clear majority. In China, **Q3 GDP came in at +4.5% y/y** (Q2: 4.3%), slightly above consensus (polls ranged from 4.4% to 4.6%).

## The fun bit

**39 years ago today, on 19 October 1987, the Dow fell 508 points, or 22.6%, to 1,738.74** — still the largest one-day percentage drop in its history. Hong Kong shut its exchange for four working days, and Tokyo fell 14.9% in a single session.

The London prologue is the one traders forget: on **Friday 16 October 1987** — 39 years ago last Friday — the Great Storm closed the London market. When it reopened on Monday it fell about 23% over two days. So if your commute was wrecked by a strike this morning, take comfort: in 1987 it was a hurricane, and it cost a quarter of the FTSE.

For scale, a 22.6% drop from Friday's Dow close would be roughly 11,750 points. Nobody needs that before the Netflix print.

Bon courage, and mind the gap on the Paris open.
