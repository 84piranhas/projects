**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Tuesday 27 October 2026 — Moody's blinks, Anthropic files, Brazil breaks**

Good morning,

Monday was a relief session. Moody's chose not to downgrade France. Anthropic's S-1 reminded everyone that AI companies can grow revenue as well as spend money. Oil eased a little. The exception was Brazil, where an election result produced the kind of price action normally reserved for emerging-markets textbooks. The Fed starts its two-day meeting today, so enjoy the calm while it lasts.

**Paris.** The CAC was carried by lower French long-end yields. **Stellantis** (+3.8%), **Hermès** (+3.2%), **STMicro** (+2.6%) and **BNP Paribas** (+2.4%) led. **GTT** fell 11.7% to €181.10. Its Q3 revenue of €183m came in about 7% below forecasts, and it only confirmed guidance. **Valeo** gained 3.3% after losing 16% over three sessions. **EssilorLuxottica** fell 3.4% and was the weakest Euro Stoxx 50 name. **Nanobiotix** rose 9.5% on trial data.

**Rest of Europe.** **Siemens Energy** rose 3.8%. **Bayer** added 3.6% on interim Nubeqa data, and JPMorgan thinks consensus for the drug is too low. **Infineon** (+3.1%) and **Aixtron** (+3.5%) joined the AI rebound. **Fraport** (+2.6%) beat expectations, helped by a one-off item. **Santander** fell 3.1% as investors reassessed its Brazil exposure. Germany's Ifo index rose to 90.2, against 90.0 expected.

**Wall Street.** Anthropic's public IPO filing (Nasdaq, ticker "ANT") lifted tech. The SOX gained 2.8%, though it's still down about 12% for the month. **Nvidia** rose about 2–2.4%, **Microsoft** 1.5% and **Tesla** 5.2% after a Barclays upgrade to Overweight (target $450). **Meta** fell about 1.9% after announcing a huge equity raise to fund AI capex. Most reports put it at up to $95bn, but AP cites $35bn, so expect clarification. M&A was busy:

- **Range Resources** jumped about 16% after EQT agreed an all-stock takeover worth more than $50bn including debt.
- **Surgery Partners** rose about 48% after agreeing a $5.7bn sale to Bon Secours Mercy Health.
- **FICO** fell about 9–14% on downgrades linked to its pricing strategy.
- Neocloud names (**CoreWeave**, **Nebius**, **Iren**) slipped after Elon Musk predicted that many of the sector's 200+ players could fail.

**Brazil.** Lula won the run-off with 51.8% of the vote. The Ibovespa fell about 13.9%, its worst day in six years, and trading was halted by a circuit breaker. US-listed **XP** fell about 50%, **Nu Holdings** about 20% and **Petrobras** about 14%.

### Bonds

- **OAT 10y:** 4.976%, down 4.5bp (Bloomberg via BNN). On Thursday it reached 5.14%, the highest since June 2002. Zonebourse has a lower close of 4.91% (-13bp). We use the Bloomberg figure.
- **Bund 10y:** 3.357%, up 1.5bp. The **Schatz** rose 2bp to 3.049% as safe-haven money left.
- **OAT–Bund spread:** 161bp, down 5bp (Zonebourse quotes 155bp). Reports describe it as the biggest one-day tightening since 2020. Last week the spread was at its widest since 2011. Moody's kept France at **Aa3, outlook negative**, and said the rating assumes a deficit-reducing 2027 budget. A fragmented parliament makes that assumption less safe than it sounds.
- **US Treasuries:** the 10y was flat at 4.987%. The 2y rose 3.2bp to 4.712% as the market kept a December hike in play, so the curve flattened slightly.

### FX

- **EUR/USD** closed at 1.1186 (-0.14%) and was around 1.1173 early this morning (AP).
- **USD/JPY** was near 157.5–157.65. The yen was the weakest G10 currency on Monday.
- **USD/BRL** rose 8.2% to 5.4027. That is the real's biggest one-day drop since 1999.

### Other assets

- **Brent (Dec)** settled at $115.81 (-0.8%) and **WTI** at $101.57 (-1.7%). Hopes of easier US–Iran tensions helped, although the Houthis said they targeted Riyadh's airport and oil sites. AP quotes Brent at $109.82 this morning. The gap with Monday's settle most likely reflects a different contract month (the December contract is near expiry), so check your own screen.
- **European gas** rose 2.8% to €96.44/MWh, a four-year high (Saxo).
- **Gold (Dec)** rose 0.8% to $4,038.40.
- **Bitcoin** was around $79,800, down about 0.4–1.2% depending on the source.

### The why, in four lines

1. **France got a reprieve, not a cure.** Avoiding a downgrade took some pressure off the OAT, and French banks and the CAC benefited.
2. **AI sentiment recovered.** Anthropic's S-1 showed strong revenue growth, which eased the "capex with no payback" worry. Meta's capital raise was a reminder of what that capex costs.
3. **Lower oil helped breakevens and risk appetite**, although Brent is still above $110.
4. **Brazil was a contained but brutal EM shock.** The damage was limited to Brazil-exposed names such as Santander, XP, Nu and Petrobras.

## The fun bit

**On this day, 27 October 1997**, the Dow fell **554.26 points (-7.18%)** to 7,161.15. It was the first time the NYSE's circuit breakers were triggered. The first 30-minute halt came at a 350-point fall. When the second threshold (550 points) was hit with only 30 minutes left, the exchange simply closed early at 15:30 (New York time). The next day the Dow recovered about 61% of the loss.

Twenty-nine years later to the day, it is São Paulo's circuit breaker in the news. The lesson hasn't changed: a pause stops the selling for a while, but it doesn't change anyone's mind. And at today's levels, 554 Dow points would be about 1%.
