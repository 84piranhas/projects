**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Tuesday 20 October 2026 — Paris burns, the spread yearns, Nasdaq returns**

Good morning,

A tale of two continents yesterday. In Europe, a weekend of violent cost-of-living protests in France sent investors running from anything with a tricolour on it: French banks were hit hard, the OAT-Bund spread widened to 147 bp, and Moody's is due to look at France on Friday. Across the Atlantic, Wall Street could not have cared less: chips ended an eight-day losing streak and the Nasdaq added more than 1%. Coffee first, then the numbers.

**Europe: risk-off, made in France.** Saturday's protests against the cost of living drew 500,000 to 800,000 people, according to figures relayed by Médiaterranée. They ended in looting and damage in several cities, with 2,397 arrests and 997 police and gendarmes injured according to the Interior Ministry. Markets read this as a political story and, behind it, a fiscal one. France still has no majority to pass its 2027 budget, and Moody's (Aa3, negative outlook) reviews the rating on Friday after the European close.

- **French banks** were the main way to sell France: **Société Générale −3.41%** (worst in the CAC), **Crédit Agricole −2.64%**, **BNP Paribas −2.48%**. **AXA** −1.66%.
- **Exposure to French consumers and property** was also sold: **Accor −3.03%**, **Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield −2.05%**.
- **Rubis +2.6%** went the other way. It plans to convert from a partnership limited by shares (SCA) into a standard public company (SA). The press notes this could ease a takeover by Bolloré; shareholders vote on 9 December.
- **Germany:** RWE −3.15%, Rheinmetall −2.79%, Deutsche Bank −2.76%, Airbus −2.25%. **DHL −0.9%** despite raising its operating profit guidance. **Vonovia −1.5%** as a possible Berlin coalition talks about socialising large housing portfolios. Zalando (+2.61%) and VW (+1.16%) were among the few gainers.
- **UK banks** sold off too: Barclays −3.5%, NatWest −2.7%, HSBC −2.1%. Miners were also weak (Anglo American −4%, Glencore −3.3%).

**US: the AI trade woke up.** The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 1.61%, ending an eight-session losing streak, its longest since 2010 (Investrade). Consumer discretionary and tech led. **Amazon +4.95%** (a Morgan Stanley top pick), **Palantir +4.36%**, **Tesla +3.74%**, **Micron +2.95%**, while SanDisk and Western Digital each gained close to 6%. Brokers helped: Intel was upgraded to Neutral by JPMorgan, CoreWeave to Buy by Goldman and Datadog to Outperform by Bernstein. **Cleveland-Cliffs +16%** after a Q3 beat. **SLN (Silence Therapeutics) more than doubled** on a $1.3bn cash bid from Merck KGaA. The Dow lagged slightly.

**After the bell:** Steel Dynamics −5.4%, Crown Holdings −5.1%, W.R. Berkley −2.4%; Zions was flat.

### Other

- **Oil:** December **Brent settled at $109.55 (−0.28%)**, just below its highest level since May. The November WTI contract, which expires soon, rose 2.2% to $103.66, after Houthi forces claimed attacks on Saudi targets, while December WTI slipped to $98.54. Goldman estimates the Brent risk premium tied to the Iran war at close to $25.
- **Gold:** December contract **$4,160 (−0.5%)**, pressured by real yields and the dollar. China's central bank officially bought 80.2 t from January to August; Julius Baer thinks the real figure is closer to 190 t.
- **Bitcoin:** about **$82,000**, +1.4% over 24 hours (single source; treat as indicative).

**Why it moved, in three lines:** (1) France: social unrest plus no budget majority plus a Moody's review on Friday hits banks and the OAT. (2) US tech: after a week in which chips fell 11% on worries about AI spending and how it is financed, buyers came back. (3) Rates: hot inflation and $100+ oil keep the long end above 5%, and that caps any relief rally.

Shinhan Securities' base case is a one-notch cut to A1. *Our call: ~60% that Moody's moves this week, with a cut to A1 at least as likely as just another negative outlook.* Expect the OAT to trade defensively into Friday.

The spread is the number to watch: a move back towards 155–160 bp would show that stress is building.

## The fun bit

**On this day, 20 October 1987**, one day after Black Monday had wiped **22.6%** off the Dow (−508 points, still the largest one-day percentage fall in the index's history), Alan Greenspan's Fed issued a statement that was a single sentence long. It affirmed the Fed's "readiness to serve as a source of liquidity". It worked. Thirty-nine years later, the Dow is near 52,000 and nobody gets that much done in one sentence any more.

Tip for Friday: if Moody's statement is shorter than Greenspan's, sell first and read it afterwards.
