**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Thursday 22 October 2026 — The OAT hits 5%, the CAC shrugs, and Musk lowers the bar**

Good morning,

Paris managed the neat trick of rallying 1% on the same day its 10-year borrowing cost crossed 5% for the first time since 2002. Credit EssilorLuxottica, a tech bid out of the US, and a market that has decided riots are a retail problem, not an index problem. Overnight, the mood has soured again: Tesla cut its capex, Asia sold chips, and Nasdaq futures are down more than 1%. Coffee first.

**Europe.** The day's story was one of two French retailers of very different fortunes:

- **EssilorLuxottica +15.5%**, best in the Euro Stoxx 50, after Q3 revenue rose 7.2% at constant currency, ahead of forecasts and led by plain old prescription frames rather than smart glasses.
- **Carrefour −13.8%** after cutting its full-year earnings guidance, blaming riots, looting and curfews in France for higher costs and disrupted store trading. The first listed casualty of the unrest, and probably not the last.
- **Michelin −7.2%**: per Bernstein, Q3 came in weaker than the tyre maker had signalled a few weeks earlier.
- **SAP +3.5%** ahead of its numbers, alongside ASML, Siemens, Infineon and Schneider (+2% to +2.6%). After the close, SAP posted Q3 EPS of €1.96 (consensus €1.81) and revenue of €10.27bn. Its cloud backlog of €24.1bn (+26% at constant FX) came in a little short of the ~€24.5bn expected. The ADR still closed +5.3% in New York.
- Also: Randstad +11.6% (organic growth beat), Iberdrola +1.5% (another guidance upgrade), Orange −1.3% (nationwide mobile outage). In Germany, Rheinmetall (−3.2%) lagged.

**US.** Tech led a rotation out of defensives (XLK +1.7%), though the indices gave back some ground in the last hour as yields and oil ticked up.

- **SpaceX +7.8%** after a strong quarter. Elon Musk also said SpaceX and Tesla have set up special committees to discuss a possible merger. **Tesla +3.4%** in the session.
- **Super Micro** rose as much as 12% on a higher revenue forecast. Cybersecurity names (Zscaler, Okta, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike) gained 5–7% after a critical PHP flaw ("PHP2Shell") took some big sites offline for patching.
- **Coinbase and Circle +4%+** after a federal banking regulator finalised its stablecoin rules.
- **Netflix −4/5%** on a Q4 revenue forecast below consensus and a cut to its 2026 ad revenue target. **AT&T −6%**: postpaid phone adds were light, and Starlink's mobile ambitions are keeping investors up at night.

**After the bell:**

- **Tesla.** Revenue was $28.05bn (flat, in line), adjusted EPS $0.48 vs $0.45 expected, and gross margin rebounded to 19.6%. GAAP net income still fell 43%. The stock first popped, then reversed on the call when 2026 capex was cut to ~$20bn from ">$25bn", with no detail on which projects lose out. It traded around −3.3% after hours.
- **Meta.** Revenue was $64.6bn (+26%) with Q4 guidance of $73–76bn. GAAP EPS of $3.83 includes a $9.9bn legal charge tied to a settlement with 52 attorneys general; excluding it, EPS was $7.40. Capex guidance went up to $135–145bn. Meta also announced a new non-voting Class C share, distributed one-for-one, which amounts to an effective 2-for-1 split. Shares were about +2% after hours.
- **Lam Research −7.4%** after hours on a soft outlook. Texas Instruments guided strong, and IBM's sales beat estimates.

### Bonds

- **France 10y: 5.00%** (+~9bp), the first close at that level since 2002. **Bund 10y: 3.42%** (+2bp). **OAT–Bund spread ≈158bp**, which German press reports call the widest in 15 years. The drivers: Q2 debt of €3.6trn (~119% of GDP), no parliamentary majority for consolidation, and student protests that have turned into riots, with partial curfews in several cities. More demonstrations are expected on Saturday.
- **German 2y: 3.08%** (+3bp).
- **US Treasuries:** 10y ≈5.13–5.15% (+~3bp intraday, finishing near flat to slightly higher depending on the source), 2y ≈4.82%. The 2s10s spread is in the low 30s bp. Housing remains a wreck: September starts −5.2%, pending home sales at a series low, NAHB at its lowest since 2012.

### FX

- **EUR/USD ≈1.1235** (−0.04%). The euro lagged the majors as the French spread widened. It is at $1.1241 this morning.
- **GBP/USD ≈1.3395** (+0.1%) after UK September CPI came in at 3.5% (3.4% expected), firming bets on a BoE hike on 5 November.
- **USD/JPY ≈158.2**, after an intraday dip on hawkish-sounding BoJ remarks. It is at 157.74 this morning.

### Other

- **Brent ≈$108.8** (flat), **WTI ≈$97.2**. Oil swung wildly: up on attacks on Saudi oil facilities, down on news of new Russia–Ukraine talks, back up on a US embassy drawdown in the Gulf. This morning Brent is at **~$110.7 (+1.7%)** after deadly attacks in Saudi Arabia and a State Department order pulling non-emergency staff out of the kingdom.
- **Gold ≈$4,120–4,135**, roughly flat (sources differ). It is down 1.2% to ~$4,092 this morning.
- **Bitcoin** rose ~2% to ~$84,500 with tech, then gave it back overnight (~$82,900).

**Why it moved, in three lines:** Europe bought earnings and US tech momentum and decided to look past France's unrest everywhere except in French retail stocks and the OAT. In the US, a third straight up day was about rotation back into tech ahead of the megacap prints. Overnight, those prints failed to settle the "is AI capex paying off?" debate, and Tesla's capex cut tipped the scales.

## The fun bit

On **22 October 1844**, thousands of followers of the preacher William Miller waited for the end of the world, a date reached by careful calculation and great conviction. The world kept turning, and the day went down in history as **"The Great Disappointment."** The Philadelphia semiconductor index recently fell eight sessions in a row, its longest losing streak since October 2008 (per Bloomberg). Spare a thought this morning for every strategist whose year-end target needed AI to deliver right on schedule. Precise dates, as Miller learned, are a high-conviction trade.

Bonne journée, and mind the spread.
