Europe had a third straight day in the red, with miners and Alstom leading the way down. Wall Street split: the Dow dropped 1.1% and the Nasdaq rose. **Europe.** The Stoxx 600 has now fallen three sessions in a row. It closed at its lowest since 6 November, about 3% below last week's record. **Basic resources fell 4.7%, the sector's worst day since May.** Copper hit a seven-month low, Glencore lost 6.1% and Aurubis fell more than 5%. Energy shed 2.1%. In Paris, **Alstom fell about 7%**. It reported an H1 adjusted net loss of €822m, and Deutsche Bank says a large capital increase now looks likely. **Accor fell 4.2%** after agreeing to buy out the Ennismore minorities, at a price Barclays sees as rich and possibly dilutive. Deutsche Bank lost another 3.1% over a leak of internal documents. Experian gained 1.7% on H1 results and a $500m buyback, and ASML rose 1.9%.

**US.** The S&P 500 fell for a **fifth straight session**, down nearly 4% over that run, its longest losing streak since June. **Nvidia closed 2.9% lower at $150.96** on twice its usual volume. Q3 revenue was $115.8bn, more than double a year ago, and Q4 guidance of about $130bn assumes no data-centre sales to China. The market cared more about the governance overhang (interim CEO Colette Kress, Jensen Huang out since 12 November) and China exposure than about the beat. Other chips did better: AMD +5.3%, Broadcom +3.7%. **Managed care was hit for a second day** by a CMS proposal to widen Medicare Advantage audit recoveries: Humana -13.0%, UnitedHealth -10.2%, Elevance -7.6%. **Energy was the worst S&P sector (-3.5%)** as crude fell, and gold miners Newmont and Freeport lost more than 6%. Retail earnings came in well: **TJX +8.1%** and **Target +4.0%** (comps +5.6%, full-year outlook raised). **Palo Alto Networks rose about 10%** on a beat-and-raise and a $4.5bn all-stock deal for Applied Compute, and other cyber names gained with it (CrowdStrike about +8%).

### Bonds

- **US Treasuries:** the 10-year closed at **5.07% (-3bp)** and the 2-year at **4.80% (unchanged)**, so the curve flattened slightly. The 30-year was 5.41% (-5bp). *The Treasury's official table for 18 Nov was not yet published at writing time; the 17 Nov par yields were 2y 4.80% and 10y 5.10%. The 18 Nov levels are market closes reported by Investopedia and the Capp Notes recap.*
- **OAT 10y: 4.87% (-2.6bp). Bund 10y: 3.37% (-0.6bp).** These are indicative market levels from Trading Economics. **Our calculated OAT-Bund spread is about 150bp,** roughly 2bp tighter on the day. The 150bp level is still uncomfortable for Paris. LSEG pricing, as reported by Trading Economics, shows about a **91% chance of a 25bp ECB hike on 17 December**.
- The front end moved little because hikes are already fully priced. The long end rallied as oil, metals and equities all fell.

### FX

- **EUR/USD: ECB reference rate 1.0968** (18 Nov, vs 1.0989 the day before). The end-of-day level was **1.0965 (-0.09%)** on Interactive Brokers data. This morning it trades around 1.096.
- USD/JPY was near 159.3 at the close and is around 159.5 this morning, so the 160 level is close. **AUD fell up to 0.8% overnight** to 66.6 US cents and gave back its 2026 gain, after weak Australian jobs data shifted bets towards RBA cuts next year.

### Other assets

- **Gold futures fell 3.5% to about $3,729**, the lowest since September 2025, as Fed officials argued for more hikes. Silver fell 4.8% and **copper about 5%**.
- **Oil: Brent settled at $91.95 (-1.3%) and WTI at $83.26 (-3.2%).** US gasoline averages about $3.75, down from $4.30 a month ago. Crude is steady overnight while the US-Iran situation stays unresolved.
- **Bitcoin fell about 4% to around $62.3k**, down from roughly $80k two weeks ago. It is back above $63.5k this morning.

### Why it moved

The main driver was **the Fed**. The October minutes showed almost all officials expect further hikes to be appropriate. Governor Waller told CNBC he is "very, very concerned about inflation" and floated a **50bp move on 9 December**. That hit anything priced off real yields, starting with gold, then miners and long-duration hopes. **Nvidia showed the market's mood**: record numbers were not enough to offset governance and China concerns. Meanwhile **Anthropic's $83 IPO pricing**, above the $70-78 range, drew money towards a new AI name and away from the old ones. In Europe, traders cited worries that the IPO would drain liquidity, and Europe also got the copper sell-off with no Nasdaq rebound to cushion it.

## Today — what might happen

- **Anthropic opens above $83:** *our call: ~75%*. The book was reported at roughly $500bn of non-binding demand, and Nasdaq futures are bid. The real test is whether it holds the open into the close. *Our call: ~55%.* Expect selling in other AI mega-caps as investors fund their allocations.
- **Fed hikes in December:** about **98% for at least 25bp** (LSEG, via Trading Economics). CME FedWatch describes it as near-certain. We found no published probability for 50bp; *our call: ~25%*. A strong Philly Fed reading or low claims would push that higher.
- **CAC 40 opens higher:** *our call: ~65%*, given firm US and Euro Stoxx futures. Mining stocks may lag if copper does not bounce.
- **Walmart beats on EPS:** *our call: ~70%*, in line with its long record of small beats. The guidance will matter more to the market than the quarter.
