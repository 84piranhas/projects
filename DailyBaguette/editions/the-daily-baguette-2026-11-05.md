**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Thursday 5 November 2026 — Blue wave, black gold: the Dow's best morning-after in 44 years, and the Old Lady's turn to hike**

Good morning. Washington delivered a clean Democratic sweep of Congress, Trump accepted the result, and investors did what they always do when uncertainty leaves the room: bought nearly everything except oil, defence and, unusually, the AI trade. Now the Bank of England is expected to spoil the mood with a rate hike at 13:00.

### The why

Three moves did most of the work.

1. **Clarity.** A decisive result removed the contested-election tail risk, so the bid was broad.
2. **Oil.** The market read the sweep as added pressure to end the Iran war, with Trump saying there were no immediate plans to step up strikes. Some sources give the credit to a circulating peace draft and Qatar's mediation rather than the vote. Lower crude cooled rate-hike fears, helped yields fall and lifted gold through a softer dollar.
3. **Rotation.** Regulatory fear hit AI and data-centre names, while health care and renewables gained on the new congressional arithmetic.

One caution: physical risk in Hormuz has not gone away, with four attacks reported between 30 October and 2 November. The market priced hope, not safer shipping.

## Today — what might happen

- **BoE hikes 25bp to 4.00%:** 95.3% on centralbank.watch; Polymarket's hike bucket was at about 98.5 cents. Both date from 3 November. The hike would be the first of this cycle, driven by energy and 3.5% CPI.
- **The vote:** Goldman Sachs expects 7-2. *Our call: ~65% for at least seven votes in favour.* Oil's fall gives the doves a fresh talking point, so the guidance matters more than the move. Markets price several further hikes, which many economists consider excessive, and a cautious tone would weigh on sterling.
- **A Fed hike in December:** about 78-79% on CME FedWatch, as cited by Multibagg and Trading Economics; 71.3% on centralbank.watch as of 3 November. *Our call: this drifts lower if crude holds below $100, but payrolls has the last word.*
- **Brent stays below $100 today:** *our call: ~85%.* It was quoted around $93-97 overnight. An Iranian official says the sides remain far apart, and unverified IRGC claims of burning tankers in Hormuz are the obvious upside risk.
- **CAC 40 closes above Tuesday's breakout area, around 8,107:** *our call: ~70%.* The earnings slate is heavy, and yesterday's gain leaves room for profit-taking.
- **OAT auction clears without drama:** *our call: ~80%.* Lower yields help demand, though 10-year levels near 4.8% are hardly a comfort for the Trésor.

## The fun bit

Yesterday's Dow rise of 3.03% was billed as its best post-midterm session in 44 years. Before anyone frames it, the record-holder remains 1982, when the session after the vote brought +4.3% for the Dow and +3.9% for the S&P. The S&P's +1.58% yesterday did not even beat the +2.1% of November 2018.

The statistic that will be quoted at every client lunch this week: the S&P 500 has risen in the six months after each of the 13 midterms since 1974, by about 12% on average, excluding dividends. Thirteen out of thirteen is a remarkable record. It is also a sample smaller than a decent Paris bakery's morning queue.
