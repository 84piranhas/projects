**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Thursday 29 October 2026 — Warsh holds, Alphabet folds, Lagarde's turn**

Good morning,

The Fed held, with two members voting to hike. Europe did nothing at all, to the second decimal place. Then after the bell Alphabet missed for the first time in almost four years and gave back roughly $400bn of market value in an hour. Today it's the ECB's turn, with a hold expected, and there's a heavy stack of earnings to get through. Coffee first.

The S&P 500 rose for a third straight session, and the Philly semis index gained 1.75%. Energy led the S&P sectors and real estate and utilities lagged. US closing levels match across two sources (Motley Fool, CappNotes).

**Paris.** The CAC 40 closed exactly flat, but the stock moves underneath were large:
- **Stellantis +15%**, top of the index. Its Q3 update showed smaller losses, a better operating margin and every 2026 target confirmed. Buyers had plainly been waiting for that.
- **Danone +2.6%**, on 4% organic growth that beat expectations.
- **Worldline +4.1%**, after a quarterly beat.
- **Dassault Systèmes −4.5%**, the worst in the CAC. It raised its annual targets, but analysts found the raise too small.
- **Saint-Gobain −1.8%**, on in-line sales after four days of gains. **Rémy Cointreau −2.5%**, as investors took profits despite a Q2 beat.

**Elsewhere in Europe.** Heineken rose 1.9% on market-share gains, led by Asia. Symrise fell 9.8% because its outlook disappointed, even though Q3 growth beat.

**Wall Street.** Vertiv rose ~17%: Q3 sales grew 61%, orders excluding acquisitions more than doubled and full-year guidance went up. Oracle gained ~11% after its investor day, and Wendy's added ~14% on Trian's non-binding $7.35/share proposal. Genmab jumped ~28% on Phase 3 data in head-and-neck cancer showing a 50% cut in the risk of death. On the other side, F5 fell ~20% on its FY27 guide and a downgrade, and Enphase dropped ~16% because Q4 guidance fell short of Q3 revenue. Enphase blamed soft residential demand, higher rates and the expired Section 25D tax credit.

**After the bell.**
- **Alphabet −10%** in extended trading. It missed on both revenue and earnings, its first double miss in nearly four years, and posted negative free cash flow of about $9bn even with $30.7bn of Google Cloud revenue. AI-capex anxiety now has a poster child.
- **Microsoft +2.6%**: revenue of $92.3bn beat the $90.7bn estimate, and Azure grew 48%.
- **ServiceNow −18%**, after an adjusted EPS miss of 2 cents. **Fortinet −26%** on gross margin. **Sprouts −22%** after missing on every line.
- **Chipotle +7%**, with same-store sales up 2.1% against 1.5% expected.

### Bonds

- **Fed:** rates held at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland's Beth Hammack dissented, both in favour of a hike. The statement still describes inflation as elevated. Chair Warsh said data will decide December, and he announced that one of the eight 2027 meetings will be a strategy session with no rate vote.
- **UST 10y:** 5.04% (+5bp) per CappNotes, while Motley Fool has 5.02%. Either way it is back above 5%. The **2y** was roughly flat at 4.73%, so 2s10s steepened to about 31bp. In Asian trading the 2y fell ~6bp and the curve steepened again (Reuters).
- **OAT 10y:** 4.94% (+2.5bp). **Bund 10y:** 3.38% (+2.6bp). The OAT–Bund spread is about **156bp**, essentially unchanged and still near its widest since the euro crisis (Reuters via Boursorama).

### FX

- **EUR/USD:** 1.1147 at the European close (+0.22%, Reuters) and 1.1155 by the New York close (+0.30%, CappNotes). The DXY fell 0.16% to 102.16.
- **NZD:** the kiwi lost about 1% to $0.5626 after the RBNZ unexpectedly held. The **Bank of Canada** raised rates for the first time since July 2023, as expected, and signalled more hikes could follow.

### Other

- **Brent:** $116.26 at the European close (+2.1%) and $115.32 at the US settle per CappNotes. WTI rose 2.9% to $102.78. In Asia this morning Brent eased to ~$114.9 after a report that VP JD Vance wrote to Iran's Mohsen Rezaei to revive peace talks.
- **European gas** went above **€100/MWh** for the first time since 2022. Middle East tensions and thin EU storage ahead of winter are behind it.
- **Gold:** December futures fell 0.5% to $4,051.60.
- **Bitcoin:** ~$81,255 (+0.8%).

**The why, in brief.** The Fed held, but the two hawkish dissents told markets that December is a live meeting. Long yields rose and the front end barely moved, so the curve steepened. With oil above $115, gas above €100 and the 10y Treasury above 5%, any reassurance was welcome. Stellantis supplied some in Paris, and AI-infrastructure names supplied it in New York until Alphabet reported. Europe spent the day waiting on the Fed, and the CAC finished exactly flat.

## Today — what might happen

- **ECB holds at 2.50%.** Markets have this almost fully priced, and 70 of 73 economists in Reuters' poll expect a hold. The real question is December: 64 of 73 expect a 25bp hike then, when new staff projections arrive. *Our call:* if Lagarde says nothing on December, some of that hike gets priced out and the euro softens a little. ~55% she sticks to "meeting by meeting."
- **Fed December hike:** about **80% priced** per Reuters this morning. A hot core PCE print (0.4% m/m or more) would make that close to a done deal. *Our call:* ~30% chance of an upside surprise to the 0.3% consensus.
- **Alphabet contagion.** *Our call:* ~65% that the Nasdaq underperforms the Dow again today. Amazon's capex line tonight decides whether the AI-spending scare is a one-day event or something longer.
- **CAC 40.** *Our call:* a modestly lower open. US tech weakness and a busy earnings morning outweigh SocGen's beat. Watch the OAT: a 10y yield back above 5% would weigh on French banks, record profits or not.
- **Oil.** The Vance–Iran letter is the only de-escalation headline around. *Our call:* Brent stays in a $110–118 range until there's something more concrete than a letter.

## The fun bit

Ninety-seven years ago today was **Black Tuesday**, 29 October 1929. The Dow fell **11.73%** to close at **230.07**, AT&T lost 38% and GE lost 58%. The market only bottomed in July 1932, at 41.22.

Yesterday the Dow closed at 52,475.10, about **228 times** its Black Tuesday close. Before anyone gets carried away, that's a price index, so no dividends and no inflation adjustment. Still, it's a decent lesson in perspective for anyone staring at Alphabet's after-hours chart. Selling in a panic has a famously bad long-term record. So does buying at ten times the previous decade's prices, which is what investors were doing in 1929. Pick your lesson according to your book.
