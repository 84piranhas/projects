**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Thursday 12 November 2026 — Chips rally by day, Nvidia boardroom drama by night**

Good morning,

Paris was closed for Armistice Day but the Bourse wasn't, and the few who turned up got a good session: Europe closed at a record, led by AI names and a stellar Siemens Energy. Wall Street followed with a CoreWeave-powered tech rally. Then, after the US close, Nvidia's board became the story — and futures are now giving back part of yesterday's gains. Coffee first, then the details.

**Europe.** Volumes were thin in Paris, but tech rose ~2.5% across the region on the back of CoreWeave's results (see below) and renewed AI appetite, helped by reports that Anthropic is preparing an IPO. The STOXX 600 closed at a record, a hair below its intraday high.

- **Siemens Energy +10.8%**: record fiscal 2026, a higher 2027 margin target and plans to more than triple the dividend. Siemens Gamesa reached break-even, which is news in itself.
- **Chips**: Soitec +11.9%, Aixtron +8%, ASM International +7%, Infineon +6.3%, BESI +5.9%, STMicro +5.2%, ASML ~+3%.
- **Electrification**: Schneider Electric +3.0%, Legrand +2.2%.
- **TotalEnergies +1.5%**, helped by firmer crude.
- **Defence was hit**: CSG −21.6% after cutting its operating-margin guidance; Rheinmetall −6.9%. In Paris, Thales slipped ~1%.
- Laggards on the CAC: Stellantis −3.3%, EssilorLuxottica −2.4%, URW −1.7%.

**US.** CoreWeave **+24.7%** led the day: Q3 revenue of $3.7bn (+168%) and a raised FY26 outlook ($13–13.3bn revenue). It pulled the whole AI chain higher. Micron, Sandisk and SK Hynix each gained more than 10%, Coherent ~+18%, Lumentum +12%, and Nvidia **+2.9% to $173.73** after a two-day slide. Apple briefly hit a record above $355, worth about $5.2tn. The Nasdaq has now erased its post-midterm losses, and the S&P 500 is 2.6% below its October record. On the downside: Lululemon, Clorox and Airbnb, the last after reports that OpenAI is moving into its territory.

**The after-hours bombshell.** The NYT reported that Jensen Huang had a second affair, this one with a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him. The WSJ reported that the board is preparing to oust him. Reuters says the board met late Wednesday; at the time of writing no outcome has been announced. Nvidia fell **8.6%** after hours (as much as −10% at one point), coming on top of the existing probe into a relationship with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. Baird downgraded the stock to Neutral; Morgan Stanley removed it from its Top Picks list. Reuters notes there is no clear succession plan; Ian Buck and CFO Colette Kress are mentioned as possible interim CEOs.

### Bonds

- **Treasuries: no cash trading** (Veterans Day). Futures imply a 10-year around 4.98% this morning, still close to 5% after Tuesday's hot CPI (headline +0.4% m/m, core +0.3%, both 0.1pp above consensus). The 30-year is near 5.32%.
- **Bund 10y: 3.32%** (+2bp); **Schatz 2y: 3.12%** (~+4bp). Price action was calmer after Tuesday's jump.
- **OAT 10y: ~4.79–4.80%**, putting the **OAT–Bund spread at ~147–149bp** (Zonebourse quotes 149bp; our calculation from Trading Economics data gives ~147bp). French fiscal and political risk is still priced in.
- **BTP 10y** ~4.35%, i.e. ~103bp over Bunds.
- Rates backdrop: ECB chief economist Philip Lane said again that a December hike may be warranted, and Lagarde's planned early departure is now in the mix. In the US, the market fully prices a 25bp Fed **hike** in December.

### FX

- **EUR/USD ~1.108** this morning (1.1091 Wednesday evening per AP), close to its 17-month low around 1.110, weighed down by Fed hike pricing. The DXY is at **103.08, its highest since April 2025** (Reuters).
- **USD/JPY 159.7**, edging toward 160, where intervention risk rises.

### Other

- **Brent**: rose to ~$94 (+2.7%) Wednesday evening on a Strait of Hormuz shipping attack and reports that Tehran rejected a US overture; it is back to **~$91.6 (−1.2%)** this morning. WTI is around $85.3–85.5.
- **Gold ~$3,975–3,988**, struggling after Tuesday's ~3% drop. A rising dollar and higher real rates are a poor mix for gold.
- **Bitcoin ~$73,700**, near three-month lows, with spot ETF outflows.

**Why it moved, in short:** strong corporate results (CoreWeave, Siemens Energy) beat macro worries for one day, despite a hawkish Fed and ECB. That rally relies on a narrow group of AI winners, and the sector's flagship now faces a governance crisis.

## Today — what might happen

- **Nvidia leadership change today?** No published odds. *Our call: ~50/50 that the board announces a decision (departure, leave or an interim structure) before the US open on Friday.* Wedbush's Dan Ives values the stock at about $300 with Huang and about $225 without. Whatever happens, expect volatility to spread across Nasdaq-heavy books and to European chip names (ASML, ASMI, BESI, Infineon, Soitec, STMicro), after yesterday's gains.
- **Fed in December:** the market fully prices a 25bp hike and assigns a small probability to 50bp (Bloomberg, futures pricing). With four or five Fed speakers today, *our call: >70% that at least one of them leaves the 50bp option open.*
- **30-year auction:** after CPI and with the long end around 5.3%, demand will be watched closely. *Our call: tail risk is higher than usual, ~55% chance of a tail.*

- **Europe:** *our call: ~65% that the CAC closes lower on the day.* Siemens and Allianz may hold up the DAX, but AI and luxury names are exposed if the Nasdaq sells off.

## The fun bit

**On this day, 12 November 1990**, Tim Berners-Lee and Robert Cailliau, two CERN researchers, submitted their formal proposal for a "WorldWideWeb" hypertext project, a few kilometres from Geneva. The budget was modest and the initial goal was simply to help physicists share documents. Thirty-six years later, that infrastructure carries $5tn market caps, an IPO said to value an AI lab at $1.4tn and, yesterday, a boardroom drama unfolding in real time. Proof that the best ideas in finance are often born on the Franco-Swiss border, preferably over lunch.
