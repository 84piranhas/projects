**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Wednesday 28 October 2026 — Chips up, Boeing down, Warsh next**

Good morning,

Tuesday was a day for silicon and not for steel: chipmakers on both sides of the Atlantic went on a buying spree, Boeing hit an air pocket, and oil leaked lower on Middle East diplomacy. Today the main course is served in three parts: the Fed at 19:00 Paris time, then Alphabet and Microsoft after the US close. Pace yourselves.

*Euro Stoxx 50: dpa-AFX gives 6,164.69 (+0.79%); finanzen.net shows 6,168.64 (+0.86%), probably a later print. The US closes match across TheStreet and TradingKey.*

- **Semis lead the dance.** **STMicroelectronics** jumped 7.1% to top the CAC 40. In Amsterdam, **BESI** gained 11.4%, ASM International 5% and ASML 2.8%. In Frankfurt, **Infineon** rose about 4.8% and **Siemens Energy** about 4.4%. The trigger was another round of AI-capex headlines: Meta plans to raise $95bn for computing power, and on Wall Street a $63bn financing facility to buy Broadcom AI chips for lease to Anthropic sent **Broadcom** up 4% and **CoreWeave** up nearly 8%. Anthropic's IPO filing on Monday was still feeding the AI mood. Also up in Paris: EssilorLuxottica (+3.9%), ArcelorMittal (+3.3%), Schneider (+3.0%).
- **Laggards.** **Mercedes-Benz** fell nearly 4% after cutting some annual targets, citing China and higher Q4 costs. **HSBC** lost 5.9% in London (pre-tax profit +32% to $9.6bn, but revenue disappointed on slower wealth management), so the FTSE fell 0.76%. Carrefour (-3.5%), Pernod Ricard (-1.9%) and L'Oréal (-1.4%) brought up the rear of the CAC. Porsche AG went the other way, up almost 4% on results that JPMorgan called solid.
- **US earnings.** **Boeing** fell 10.7% after saying it will miss its 2026 737 delivery target and that the production ramp is delayed. **UPS** gained 5.75% on a beat and a raised outlook. **Pentair** (+11.8%), **SoFi** (~+11%) and **Bloom Energy** (+7.8%) were rewarded for beats. **Xylem** and **Hilton** were punished for misses and guidance cuts. **Cintas** fell 7.1% on reports that the FTC will sue to block its UniFirst deal. GM rose nearly 5% after announcing it will move its listing to Nasdaq. Coca-Cola beat but slipped 1.6% on soft North American volumes.
- **The Dow barely moved (+0.03%).** Boeing's slide by itself was enough to offset most of the tech gains.

**Bonds**

- **OAT 10y:** ~4.90–4.94%, stable. **Bund 10y:** ~3.33–3.35%, a touch lower. **OAT-Bund spread:** 157–159 bp, which is where France's fiscal worries now sit. Boursorama's close gives 4.90% / 3.33% / 157 bp. Idéal Investisseur (Banque de France / Bundesbank data) gives 4.94% / 3.35% / 159.4 bp. On top of that, French consumer confidence fell in October to its lowest level since 2013 (dpa-AFX).
- **UST 10y:** ~4.99%, flat (late-session reads of 4.986–4.993%; the Fed's official H.15 print for the 27th is not out yet). Fed funds stand at 3.75–4.00% after September's hike, and the curve flattened slightly ahead of the FOMC.

**FX**

- **EUR/USD:** ~1.1125–1.1135, against an ECB fixing of 1.1188 on Monday. That is a decline of roughly 0.5% and near its lowest level since April 2025. Three things are weighing on the euro: French fiscal stress, weak growth and expensive energy. The **DXY** rose 0.35% to 102.32. USD/JPY was at 157.95 and GBP/USD at 1.3313.
- **Overnight:** the **kiwi** fell more than 1% after the RBNZ unexpectedly held rates (Business Recorder).

**Other**

- **Brent** settled at **$113.88** (-$1.93, -1.7%) and **WTI** at **$99.90** (-1.8%), back below $100. Talks between Iran and Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials eased supply fears, but Houthi attacks on Saudi assets and disruption around Hormuz keep prices near six-month highs. Brent was back at ~$114.4 in Asia this morning. *(TradingKey shows a Brent figure of $109.73 that matches neither the settlement nor Boursorama's ~$114. We have not used it.)*
- **Gold:** +1.1% to ~$4,060/oz, helped by lower oil easing inflation fears. **Bitcoin:** +0.95% to ~$80,900.

**The why, in three sentences.** The market is still paying for AI capex, and every new financing deal reads as demand for chips. Europe's semiconductor names had been neglected for weeks and rebounded hardest. Lower oil took some heat out of the inflation trade, but not enough to move yields ahead of a hawkish-leaning Fed and Thursday's ECB.

## Today — what might happen

- **Fed holds:** ~90% per CME FedWatch (hike ~7–10%, depending on the update). The real question is the tone. September CPI came in at 3.7% y/y, six days before the US election. CIBC expects "a few dissents in favour of a hike" if the Fed pauses. *Our call: ~60% chance of at least one hawkish dissent, which would push December hike pricing higher.*
- **BoC hikes 25 bp:** rate futures imply ~75%, and 19 of 34 economists in the Reuters poll expect a hike. A hold would knock CAD sharply lower.
- **Big Tech reaction:** we expect Azure growth and the capex numbers to drive the move more than the EPS figure. Benzinga flags options pricing a swing of roughly $184bn in Microsoft's market value. *Our call: ~55% chance the Nasdaq 100 futures are higher at Thursday's European open, given how much AI optimism is already in prices.*
- **French banks:** BNP opens the sector's reporting season in Paris with the OAT spread near 160 bp, so expect questions on sovereign exposure. *Our call: the spread, not the trading line, will drive the share price.*
- **EUR/USD:** a hawkish Warsh could push it towards the 1.1100 support, a level cited by Tradingpedia. *Our call: ~40% chance of a test today.*

## The fun bit

Today is the **97th anniversary of "Black Monday", 28 October 1929**. The market fell **12.8%** in a single session. On "Black Tuesday" the next day, more than 16 million shares changed hands and the Dow dropped about another 12%, closing at 198 (Britannica). For scale, yesterday's Dow close of 52,377 is roughly 265 times that level, and the day's 16 million shares would be a rounding error on a busy Nasdaq morning. Hold the 1929 comparison until your AI-capex models also come with ticker tape.
