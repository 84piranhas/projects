**Subject: The Daily Baguette — Wednesday 11 November 2026 — Paris shrugs off a hot CPI, Wall Street doesn't**

Good morning,

Armistice Day in France, Veterans Day in the US, yet nobody has called a truce on inflation. Paris trades as usual today (holiday or not), the US cash bond market takes the day off, and equities on both sides of the Atlantic must digest a US CPI print that put the Treasury 10-year back on the 5% handle. **Europe — the oil dividend.** The CAC 40 posted its best close since 8 September, carried by a slide in crude below the $90 mark intraday as Washington talked up diplomacy over further strikes on Iran. Energy-hungry names led: **ArcelorMittal** +2.96% (top of the CAC), **Saint-Gobain** +1.87%. **Renault** added ~2.2% after Jefferies upgraded to Buy (target €34 from €30), citing confirmed 2026 guidance (operating margin ≥5.5%) and no direct China exposure — still ~17% down year-to-date, so plenty of room for redemption. **Safran** +2.26% on a Bernstein target hike to €390. On the SBF 120, **Edenred** (+6.3%) and **Pluxee** (+4.8%) cheered a favourable Brazilian court ruling. On the losing side, **Stellantis** −3.4% and **Dassault Systèmes** −1.9%. Elsewhere, **Infineon** gained ~1% on annual numbers with in-line guidance, and **Marks & Spencer** +3.5% after its investor day. London lagged (FTSE ~−0.3%).

**US — inflation plus a very bad week for Nvidia.** October CPI came in at +0.4% m/m (consensus +0.3%), core +0.3% (consensus +0.2%) — the third straight 0.3% core print. No year-on-year figure was published, a lingering scar from the 2025 government shutdown. Tech was the only sector down more than 1% (−1.13%), and roughly 61% of US issues declined.

The headline act was **Nvidia**, −5.47% (−7% at the intraday low), its market value briefly dipping below $4 trillion. A shareholder suit alleges the company and its CEO misled investors over a personal relationship with a former executive of a firm described as linked to Chinese intelligence; President Trump said the FBI was looking into it, and the board's independent directors have hired outside counsel. These are allegations only — but the stock is now down roughly a quarter in a month. **Microsoft** slipped ~2–3% after Anthropic unveiled Claude workplace tools aimed squarely at Office territory, on the day Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow (825m shares at $70–78). **GE Vernova** −5.4% after SoftBank booked a $3.7bn charge on AI data-centre power equipment; **GFL Environmental** −9.2% after its board rejected a ~$22bn take-private bid led by Blackstone/KKR. Earnings winners: **Take-Two** +6.3% (GTA VI pre-orders well ahead of plan), **AAON** +19%, **ZipRecruiter** +54%. Losers: **Hims & Hers** −16.6% (EBITDA guide cut), **Digital Turbine** −36%.

After the bell: **CoreWeave** +2.6% (Q3 revenue $3.66bn, vs $1.37bn a year earlier), **Costco** +1.6% on a $30/share special dividend (~$13.3bn).

## The fun bit

Today is the 108th anniversary of the 1918 Armistice, which took effect at 11:00 on the 11th day of the 11th month — the original "eleventh hour" deadline. Fitting, then, that markets spent yesterday negotiating their own ceasefire: oil traders priced peace in the Gulf while bond traders declared war on the Fed's 2% target.

And in the "nice problem to have" department: Costco is handing shareholders a **$30-per-share special dividend**, about **$13.3bn** in total, with a buyback reauthorised through 2030. Some retailers fight for market share; Costco simply mails it back to its owners in bulk packaging.
